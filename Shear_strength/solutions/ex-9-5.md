## Statement

> The figure shows the results of three consolidated-drained triaxial tests on a dense sand (cell pressures 10, 20 and 40 kPa). Find: (a) the peak friction angle, and (b) the critical state friction angle.

The figure has two panels against axial strain $\varepsilon_a$ from 0 to 25 %: deviator stress $\sigma_d$ (0 to 100 kPa, grid every 10 kPa with minor lines every 2 kPa) and dilation $-\varepsilon_v$ (−2 to 8 %). The curves are red (10 kPa), blue (20 kPa) and black (40 kPa). Dashed guide lines at each peak and dotted lines at each plateau are drawn on click.

## Given / Find

- Three CD tests, $\sigma'_3 = 10,\ 20,\ 40\ \mathrm{kPa}$, with $\sigma_d = \sigma_1 - \sigma_3$ and $-\varepsilon_v$ plotted against $\varepsilon_a$
- **Find:** (a) $\phi'_p$; (b) $\phi'_c$

## Assumptions

The test is drained, so no excess pore pressure develops during shear and the effective stresses equal the total stresses. No back pressure is mentioned, so $\sigma'_3$ is the cell pressure.

The sand is clean and has no cementation, so $c' = 0$ and each envelope is the best line through the origin tangent to the three Mohr circles. The best tangent minimizes the misfit between each circle's radius $t$ and its distance to the line, $s\sin\phi'$, which gives $\sin\phi' = \sum s\,t / \sum s^2$ with $s = (\sigma'_1+\sigma'_3)/2$ and $t = \sigma_d/2$. On the slide the tangent is drawn by eye on equal-scale axes; the formula checks it.

The critical state is taken as the plateau of $\sigma_d$ at the end of the tests, $\varepsilon_a$ between 20 and 25 %, where the volume no longer changes.

Values are read from the plotted curves to the nearest kPa. A reading error of $\pm 1\ \mathrm{kPa}$ changes the angles by about $\pm 0.5^\circ$.

## Solution

Each test gives one Mohr circle at failure. For a sand with $c' = 0$ the envelope passes through the origin and $\sin\phi' = (\sigma'_1 - \sigma'_3)/(\sigma'_1 + \sigma'_3)$, which in terms of the deviator stress is $\sin\phi' = sigma_{d,f}/(2\sigma'_3 + \sigma_{d,f})$.

1. Peak readings — from the $\sigma_d$ curves: $\sigma_{d,p} = 39\ \mathrm{kPa}$ at $\varepsilon_a \approx 3\ \%$ (10 kPa), $\sigma_{d,p} = 56\ \mathrm{kPa}$ at $\varepsilon_a \approx 4\ \%$ (20 kPa), $\sigma_{d,p} = 95\ \mathrm{kPa}$ at $\varepsilon_a \approx 6.5\ \%$ (40 kPa)
2. Critical readings — plateau of each curve between 20 and 25 %: $\sigma_{d,c} = 20,\ 41,\ 82\ \mathrm{kPa}$
3. Major principal stress at failure — $\sigma'_{1f} = \sigma'_3 + \sigma_{d,f}$. Peak: $49,\ 76,\ 135\ \mathrm{kPa}$. Critical: $30,\ 61,\ 122\ \mathrm{kPa}$
4. Mohr's circles — one circle per test from $\sigma'_3$ to $\sigma'_1$, drawn on axes with the same scale (`ex-9-5-mohr-peak.svg` above `ex-9-5-mohr-critical.svg`, revealed on click: the two principal stresses of a test, its circle, three times, then the tangent)
5. (a) Peak envelope — best tangent through the origin to the three peak circles: $s = 29.5,\ 48,\ 87.5$ and $t = 19.5,\ 28,\ 47.5\ \mathrm{kPa}$, so $\sin\phi'_p = \dfrac{\sum s\,t}{\sum s^2} = \dfrac{6075.5}{10830.5} = 0.561$ and $\mathbf{\phi'_p \approx 34^\circ}$. Each circle alone would give $41.4^\circ$, $35.7^\circ$ and $32.9^\circ$: the peak envelope is curved, and $\phi'_p$ falls as $\sigma'_3$ rises, together with the dilation at the end of the test ($6.9,\ 3.7,\ 1.5\ \%$)
6. (b) Critical state friction angle — tangent through the origin to the three critical circles. They all touch the same line: $\sin\phi'_c = \dfrac{\sigma_{d,c}}{2\sigma'_3 + \sigma_{d,c}} = \dfrac{41}{81} = 0.506$ (and $0.500$, $0.506$ for the other two), best fit $0.506$, so $\mathbf{\phi'_c \approx 30^\circ}$. The zero-intercept fit keeps $\phi'_p$ above $\phi'_c$, as expected for a dense sand
