## Statement

> The test setup has the dimensions shown below. Calculate: (a) the pressure head, (b) elevation head, (c) total head, and (d) head loss at points B, C, D, and F in cm of water.

## Given / Find

- Geometry read from `FiguresGeneral/example_new.png`, as a chain of dimensions down the column: $A$ to $B$ = 5 cm, $B$ to $C$ = 15 cm, $C$ to $D$ = 12.5 cm, $D$ to $E$ = 7.5 cm, $E$ to $F$ = 5 cm
- $A$ is the free water surface in the upper chamber; $C$ is the top of the soil; $F$ is the base of the soil; the datum line passes through $E$ and coincides with the downstream water surface
- Standpipes $b$, $c$, $d$ and $f$ tap points $B$, $C$, $D$ and $F$
- **Find:** $h_w$, $z$, $h$ and the head loss at $B$, $C$, $D$ and $F$

## Assumptions

The datum is the horizontal line drawn through $E$, which is also the downstream water surface. Taking it there is what puts $F$ at a **negative** elevation head, $z_F = -5\ \mathrm{cm}$, and that negative number is the single most instructive thing in this example. Any other datum shifts every $z$ and every $h$ by the same constant and leaves every head loss unchanged.

The soil occupies the full column from $C$ down to $F$, so $L_{soil} = 12.5 + 7.5 + 5 = 25\ \mathrm{cm}$. The figure shows the hatched soil block starting at $C$ and ending at $F$, with $D$ and $E$ inside it.

Flow is downward, driven by the difference between the upper free water surface at $z = 40\ \mathrm{cm}$ and the downstream water surface at the datum.

Above $C$ the water is free standing water, so it loses no head. All 40 cm of head loss occurs inside the soil.

The soil is uniform, so the total head falls linearly with depth through it.

## Solution

Head is lost only where water has to move through soil, so the route is: fix the total head at the two ends of the soil, get one gradient from them, then walk down the column. Elevation heads come straight from the dimension chain, pressure heads from $h_w = h - z$.

1. Elevation heads — measuring from the datum at $E$: $z_A = 40$, $z_B = 35$, $z_C = 20$, $z_D = 7.5$, $z_F = -5\ \mathrm{cm}$
2. Boundary total heads — the upper free surface gives $h = z_A + 0 = 40\ \mathrm{cm}$; the downstream water surface sits on the datum, so the head at the exit is $0\ \mathrm{cm}$
3. Free water above the soil — standing water loses no head, so $h_B = h_C = 40\ \mathrm{cm}$, and the head loss down to $C$ is zero
4. Hydraulic gradient through the soil — $i = \dfrac{\Delta h}{L_{soil}} = \dfrac{40 - 0}{25} = \mathbf{1.6}$ (dimensionless)
5. Total head at $D$ — $D$ lies $12.5\ \mathrm{cm}$ below the top of the soil, so $h_D = 40 - (1.6)(12.5) = \mathbf{20\ \mathrm{cm}}$
6. Total head at $F$ — $F$ is at the base of the soil, $25\ \mathrm{cm}$ below $C$, so $h_F = 40 - (1.6)(25) = \mathbf{0\ \mathrm{cm}}$, which returns the exit condition and confirms the gradient
7. Pressure heads — $h_w = h - z$ at every point
8. Summary — with $\Delta h$ measured from the previous point in the list, and the cumulative loss measured from the upper surface at $A$:

| Point | $h_w$ (cm) | $z$ (cm) | $h$ (cm) | $\Delta h$ from previous (cm) | $\Delta h$ cumulative (cm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B | 5 | 35 | 40 | 0 | 0 |
| C | 20 | 20 | 40 | 0 | 0 |
| D | 12.5 | 7.5 | 20 | 20 | 20 |
| F | 5 | -5 | 0 | 20 | 40 |

Every value recomputed independently in Python, and the geometry was read off the rasterised figure rather than guessed. The solution closes on itself in two independent ways. First, $h_F$ computed from the gradient comes out at exactly $0\ \mathrm{cm}$, which is the exit boundary condition that was never used to derive $i$. Second, every pressure head in the table is positive and equals the height the corresponding standpipe rises above its tapping point, which is what the figure draws: $b$ and $c$ stand at the level of the upper surface, $d$ stands at the elevation of $C$, and $f$ stands on the datum. Units check on every step. The values reproduce the professor's spreadsheet on the following slide, including its head-loss column, which is the incremental one.

Note on $E$: it sits inside the soil at $z = 0$, so its total head is $8\ \mathrm{cm}$, not zero. The datum passing through $E$ makes its **elevation** head zero, not its total head. $E$ is not among the points asked for, and this distinction is exactly why.

## Scaffolding decisions

Step 1 is blue on every $z$. The dimension chain is arithmetic, but choosing the sign of $z_F$ is a decision, and it is the decision this example exists to teach.

Step 2 is black on the reasoning and blue on the two values.

Step 3 is blue on the two heads. Pre-printing them would give away the idea that free water loses no head, which is the point being tested.

Step 4 is black on the equation, blue on $L_{soil}$ and the substitution, green on $i$. $L_{soil}$ is blue because the length of the flow path is the quantity students get wrong here, usually by using the distance between the two water surfaces instead of the thickness of the soil.

Steps 5 and 6 are blue on the substitution, green on the head. Step 6 is kept as a separate step even though it looks redundant, because arriving back at the exit condition is the check, and a check that is pre-printed is not a check.

The step 7–8 table is blanked by column: $z$ carried down black from step 1, $h$ blue, $h_w$ blue, both head-loss columns blue.

Red notes: that the datum choice is free but must be used consistently, and that a point below it has a negative elevation head; that standing water loses no head, so the first 20 cm of the column contributes nothing; that the flow path is the soil thickness, not the distance between water surfaces; and that $E$ has zero elevation head but not zero total head.

The note on the datum will need a `data-slide` short form; the full version explains that head *losses* are datum-independent while heads themselves are not, and that runs past the slide budget.

## Flags

The head-loss column is ambiguous in the statement. "Head loss at a point" can mean the loss incurred in reaching that point from the source, or the loss between it and the previous point. The professor's spreadsheet uses the second reading and reports 0, 0, 20, 20. The table above gives both columns so the slide does not have to choose, but if only one column fits, it should be the incremental one so the slide and the spreadsheet agree.
