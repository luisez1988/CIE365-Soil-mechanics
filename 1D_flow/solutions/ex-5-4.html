Pick the right test equation Nothing maintains the head here — the water in the standpipe falls from \(H_1\) to \(H_2\), so \(q\) changes continuously. Integrating Darcy's law over \(\Delta t\) gives the falling-head equation \(k=\dfrac{a\,L}{A\,\Delta t}\,\ln\!\dfrac{H_1}{H_2}\). What makes it falling-head is the apparatus, not the soil: nothing resupplies the standpipe.
Sort out the two areas
SymbolCross-section of…Value
\(a\) the standpipe — the water that falls 7.25 \(\unit{cm^2}\)
\(A\) the specimen — the soil water flows through 11.43 \(\unit{cm^2}\)
Swapping \(a\) and \(A\) is the classic error here. They differ by only 1.6×, so a swapped answer still looks plausible.
Head-ratio term \(\ln\!\dfrac{H_1}{H_2}=\) \(\ln\dfrac{171.4}{79.3}\) \(=\ln\) 2.161 \(=\) 0.771 A ratio, so it is dimensionless — any consistent length unit works.
Geometric / time factor \(\dfrac{a\,L}{A\,\Delta t}=\) \(\dfrac{7.25\times11.62}{11.43\times110}\) \(=\) 0.0670 \(\unit{cm/s}\) This term carries all the units. \(\Delta t\) is already in seconds, so \(k\) comes out in cm/s.
Permeability \(k=\dfrac{a\,L}{A\,\Delta t}\ln\!\dfrac{H_1}{H_2}=\) \(0.0670\times0.771\) \(=\) \(5.16\times10^{-2}\) \(\unit{cm/s}\) Same order as Example 5.3 — reasonable for the gravelly sand (SW) described in the statement.
