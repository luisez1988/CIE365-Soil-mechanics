## Statement

> Calculate the hydraulic gradient between points A and B in the figure bellow and plot the piezometric line between points C and D.

## Given / Find

- Stratigraphy read from `FiguresGeneral/artisian_condition.svg`: an SM layer at the top, a dipping CH clay band beneath it, and a sand unit below the clay that daylights on the left beneath a lake whose surface is at Elv. 200 m.
- Right-hand side: a standing water body with its surface at Elv. 100 m, resting on the SM at Elv. 80 m. This is the water level of the shallow system above the clay.
- $z_A = 65\ \mathrm{m}$ (immediately above the CH band), $z_B = 56\ \mathrm{m}$ (immediately below it).
- Pt C and Pt D both at Elv. 45 m in the lower sand, $L_{CD} = 500\ \mathrm{m}$ apart.
- **Find:** $i_{AB}$, and the piezometric line between C and D.

## Assumptions

The lower sand is confined by the CH clay on the right and is open to the lake at Elv. 200 m on the left, so its piezometric level is **Elv. 200 m** throughout. This is the reading that makes the left-hand side the recharge boundary, and it is the number that sets $h_B$, $h_C$ and $h_D$. The figure also carries an Elv. 195 m leader at the shoreline; that leader points at the top of the CH band where it outcrops, not at a water level, so it does not enter the calculation. **Confirm this**, because taking 195 m instead of 200 m as the confined head changes $i_{AB}$ from 11.1 to 10.6.

The shallow system above the clay is in equilibrium with the right-hand water body, so the total head at $A$ is fixed by the Elv. 100 m surface.

The flow path from $B$ to $A$ is taken as the vertical separation of the two points, $9\ \mathrm{m}$. The clay band dips, so its true thickness measured perpendicular to bedding is slightly less than 9 m, but $A$ and $B$ are drawn on the same vertical line and the vertical distance between them is what the question asks about.

The CH clay is treated as an aquitard, not an aquiclude. It leaks, which is why a gradient across it is worth computing at all.

## Solution

Two separate bodies of water are stacked here and each one fixes the total head of the point that sits in it: $h = z + h_w$ at both, with $h_w$ read as the height of the controlling water surface above the point. The gradient then follows from $i = \Delta h / L$.

1. Which water controls which point — $A$ sits above the CH clay, in the shallow system fed by the Elv. 100 m surface; $B$ sits below the clay, in the confined sand fed by the lake at Elv. 200 m. They are two different reservoirs, separated by the clay.
2. Head at $A$ — $h_{w,A} = 100 - 65 = 35\ \mathrm{m}$, so $h_A = z_A + h_{w,A} = 65 + 35 = 100\ \mathrm{m}$
3. Head at $B$ — $h_{w,B} = 200 - 56 = 144\ \mathrm{m}$, so $h_B = z_B + h_{w,B} = 56 + 144 = 200\ \mathrm{m}$
4. Gradient across the clay — $h_B > h_A$, so the flow is **upward**, from $B$ to $A$: $i_{BA} = \dfrac{h_B - h_A}{L_{AB}} = \dfrac{200 - 100}{9} = \mathbf{11.1}$ (dimensionless)
5. Heads at C and D — both points lie in the same confined sand, so both take the head of that unit: $h_C = h_D = 200\ \mathrm{m}$, with $h_{w,C} = h_{w,D} = 200 - 45 = 155\ \mathrm{m}$
6. Gradient and piezometric line C to D — $i_{CD} = \dfrac{200 - 200}{500} = \mathbf{0}$, so the piezometric line between C and D is a **horizontal line at Elv. 200 m** and there is **no horizontal flow** in the confined aquifer between them

## Verification

```
======== Example 5.2 - perched vs artesian ========
  hwA = 100.0-65.0 = 35.0 m ; hA = 65.0+35.0 = 100.0 m
  hwB = 200.0-56.0 = 144.0 m ; hB = 56.0+144.0 = 200.0 m
  L_AB (vertical thru CH) = 65.0-56.0 = 9.0 m
  i_BA = (200.0-100.0)/9.0 = 11.1111  -> upward, B to A
  hC = hD = 200.0 m  (same confined aquifer, fed at Elv 200)
  hwC = hwD = 155.0 m ; i_CD = 0/500 = 0.0
```

Every value recomputed independently in Python. Elevations were taken from the rasterised SVG, not guessed: the rendered figure shows $A$ on the top surface of the CH band at Elv. 65 m, $B$ on its base at Elv. 56 m, the left-hand lake surface at Elv. 200 m with the sand unit outcropping beneath it, and the right-hand water body at Elv. 100 m over SM at Elv. 80 m. Units check on every step; $i$ is dimensionless, being metres over metres. Magnitude check: $i = 11.1$ is enormous against the $i \approx 0.01$ of a typical regional aquifer, and that is the physical point of the example rather than an arithmetic error. A gradient that large is what a thin clay separating two reservoirs 100 m apart in head must produce.

## Scaffolding decisions

Step 1 is entirely black and entirely prose. It is the only step in this example that involves any judgement at all, and blanking it would turn a reading exercise into a guessing exercise. The student reads the route, then computes.

Steps 2 and 3 put blue on the substitution and on both values. The decision is *which water surface elevation to use*, and the two lines sit next to each other so the contrast is unavoidable.

Step 4 puts blue on the substitution and green on $i$. The direction of flow is a separate green word: upward. Students who get the number and miss the direction have not answered the question.

Step 6 is green on $i_{CD}$ and green on the description of the line.

Red notes: that the head at a point is set by the water body it is connected to, not by the nearest water surface on the page; that $B$ is artesian, so its pressure head is far larger than its depth below ground; that a gradient of this size is what makes thin clays worth caring about; and that zero gradient means no flow but does not mean no water pressure.

The step 4 note will need a `data-slide` short form. The full text, which explains why an upward gradient through an aquitard is the condition that drives the heave problems dealt with later in the course, runs past 200 characters.

No note may state the value of $i$; the step 4 note talks about what a large gradient implies, not about how large this one is.

## Flags

**The Elv. 200 m versus Elv. 195 m reading is the one thing to check before this goes on a slide.** Both numbers appear on the left edge of the figure within a few metres of each other. The solution above takes 200 m as the water surface of the lake and 195 m as the outcrop elevation of the CH clay, which is what the rendered figure shows and what makes the arithmetic come out at $h_B = 200\ \mathrm{m}$.

The figure labels the shallow system "Perched aquifer GW" but draws the label inside the lower sand on the left-hand side, where the sand is in fact the confined unit. That label placement will confuse students who try to read the stratigraphy from it. Worth moving in the source SVG, independently of this solution.

The vertical separation of $A$ and $B$ is 9 m, which is also the vertical thickness of the clay at that location. Those two happen to coincide here because $A$ and $B$ were placed on the two faces of the band; the solution says "distance between the points" rather than "clay thickness" so the method transfers to points placed anywhere else.
