What the well levels are The water level standing in an open well is the total head there, so \(h_A=18.4\unit{m}\) and \(h_B=17.1\unit{m}\) are used as they stand. Do not subtract elevations from these. They are already total heads, with no need to separate \(\hz\) from \(\hp\), which is the whole convenience of an open well.
Hydraulic gradient \(i=\dfrac{h_A-h_B}{L}=\) \(\dfrac{18.4-17.1}{20}\) \(=\) 0.065 Dimensionless. Answer (a).
Average linear velocity The tracer travels through the pores, so it measures \(\vs\) directly: \(\vs=\dfrac{L}{\Delta t}=\) \(\dfrac{20}{167}\) \(=\) 0.120 \(\unit{m/hr}=\) \(\ee{3.33}{-3}\) \(\unit{cm/s}\) Convert with \(\times100\unit{cm/m}\) then \(\div3600\unit{s/hr}\). Answer (b): "average linear velocity" is the hydrogeologist's name for the true seepage velocity \(\vs\) introduced on the previous slide. The straight line between the holes is the shortest path a tracer could take, so this is a lower bound.
Darcy velocity Darcy's law is written in \(\vD\), not \(\vs\), so convert first: \(\vD=n_e\,\vs=\) \(0.25\times\ee{3.33}{-3}\) \(=\) \(\ee{8.32}{-4}\) \(\unit{cm/s}\) \(n_e=0.25\) is printed on the figure, not in the statement — without it part (c) cannot be answered. Multiply, do not divide: water squeezing through the pores moves faster than the fictitious velocity spread over the whole face, so \(\vD<\vs\). And \(n_e\le n\), because dead-end pores carry no flow.
Hydraulic conductivity From \(\vD=k\,i\):   \(k=\dfrac{\vD}{i}=\) \(\dfrac{\ee{8.32}{-4}}{0.065}\) \(=\) \(\ee{1.28}{-2}\) \(\unit{cm/s}\) Answer (c), and about 11 m/day. Check it against the permeability table: clean sand, which fits a productive aquifer.
