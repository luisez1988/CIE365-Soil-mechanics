## Statement

> For the points in the figure below determine the total head, the pressure head, and the location of the **piezometric line**.

## Given / Find

- Reservoir impounded against an earth dam. Crest at Elv. 1500 ft, reservoir water surface at Elv. 1490 ft.
- Points read from the figure: $A$ and $B$ on the reservoir floor at Elv. 1450 ft, $C$ on the upstream face at Elv. 1470 ft, $D$ on the upstream face at the water line, Elv. 1490 ft.
- **Find:** $h_w$ and $h$ at $A$, $B$, $C$, $D$, and the piezometric line.

## Assumptions

The four points all lie on the wetted boundary of the reservoir, so each one is in direct contact with the standing reservoir water. No seepage has been asked about and none is computed: the dam body is not part of the flow domain in this problem.

The datum is whatever fixed datum the elevations on the figure are referenced to (a survey datum such as mean sea level). It is never stated, and it does not have to be, because every elevation on the figure is already expressed in that same system. The answers are therefore reported as elevations in feet.

The reservoir is static. Water standing against the upstream face of a dam is at rest even though the dam leaks, because the seepage velocities inside the embankment are far too small to draw down the reservoir.

## Solution

In standing water the pressure head at a point is the height of water directly above it, so $h_w = \mathrm{WS} - z$ with $\mathrm{WS}$ the water surface elevation, and the total head is $h = z + h_w$. Work point $A$ in full, then apply the same two lines to the rest.

1. Datum — elevations on the figure are already referenced to a common datum, so $z$ is read straight off the figure and no conversion is needed: $z_A = 1450\ \mathrm{ft}$
2. Pressure head at $A$ — the water column standing above $A$ reaches the reservoir surface, so $h_{w,A} = \mathrm{WS} - z_A = 1490 - 1450 = 40\ \mathrm{ft}$
3. Total head at $A$ — $h_A = z_A + h_{w,A} = 1450 + 40 = \mathbf{1490\ \mathrm{ft}}$
4. Remaining points — same two lines at each elevation:

| Point | $z$ (ft) | $h_w$ (ft) | $h = z + h_w$ (ft) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 1450 | 40 | 1490 |
| B | 1450 | 40 | 1490 |
| C | 1470 | 20 | 1490 |
| D | 1490 | 0 | 1490 |

5. Piezometric line — the piezometric line is the plot of $h$ against position, so it is a **horizontal line at Elv. 1490 ft** and it coincides with the reservoir water surface
6. Flow check — $\Delta h = 0$ between every pair of points, so $i = \Delta h / L = 0$ and **there is no flow** among $A$, $B$, $C$ and $D$

## Verification

```
  D: z=   1490 ft  hw=    0 ft  h=z+hw=   1490 ft

```

Every value recomputed independently in Python. The elevations were taken from `FiguresGeneral/Pressure_heaed.svg`, rasterised and read off the rendered figure rather than guessed: $A$ and $B$ both sit on the Elv. 1450 ft line, $C$ on the Elv. 1470 ft leader, $D$ on the Elv. 1490 ft leader at the water line. Units check on every step; all quantities are lengths in feet. The result is self-consistent in the strongest way available: four points at three different elevations return the same total head, which is exactly what hydrostatics requires.

## Scaffolding decisions

Step 1 is pre-printed in black. Nothing is computed there; it exists so the student sees that the elevations are already in a common datum before being asked to subtract anything.

Steps 2 and 3 carry blue on the substitution and on the value. The decision the student is making is *which two elevations to subtract*, and that decision is the whole of this example.

The step 4 table is blanked column by column, not cell by cell: $z$ is pre-printed black (it is read off the figure, which is bookkeeping), $h_w$ is blue, $h$ is blue. Point $D$ deserves attention because $h_w = 0$ there, and a zero is the entry students most often leave empty rather than write.

Green is on $h_A$ at step 3 and on the two conclusions at steps 5 and 6.

Red notes: that the pressure head is the height of the water column above the point and not the depth below the ground surface; that $D$ sits on the water surface so its pressure head is zero rather than undefined; that the piezometric line landing on the water surface is the signature of standing water, not a coincidence; and that equal total head means no flow, which is the point the next slide builds on.

The step 6 note must not repeat "1490 ft". It says that all four values came out equal, which is the observation, and leaves the number to the blanks above it.

No `data-slide` short forms needed; every note fits inside the ~110-character slide budget.

The legacy second slide (`EX471.svg`) spends itself on the $h$ versus $x$ plot, so part 2 of the solution should be the table plus that plot rather than more algebra.
