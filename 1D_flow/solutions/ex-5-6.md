## Statement

> The figure shows water flow through the soil specimen in a cylinder. The specimen's $k$ is $3.4 \times 10^{-4}$ cm/s. 1. Calculate total head, pressure head, and pore pressure at point A and B. 2. Draw the levels of water in standpipes. 3. Compute the amount of water flow $q$ through the specimen.

## Given / Find

- $k = 3.4 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{cm/s}$, specimen diameter $\phi = 60\ \mathrm{mm}$
- Geometry read from `FiguresGeneral/Example_tubes.svg`: inlet reservoir surface at 280 mm above the datum, outlet reservoir surface at 200 mm above it, giving the 80 mm dimension on the right of the figure
- Four tapping points spaced 60 mm apart along the axis of the inclined specimen, at elevations $z_A = 50\ \mathrm{mm}$, $z_B = 75\ \mathrm{mm}$, $z_C = 100\ \mathrm{mm}$, $z_D = 125\ \mathrm{mm}$
- **Find:** $h$, $h_w$ and $u$ at the tapping points; the standpipe levels; $q$

## Assumptions

All of the head loss occurs inside the specimen between $A$ and $D$. The connecting pipework and the end fittings are treated as frictionless, so the standpipe at $A$ reads the inlet reservoir level and the standpipe at $D$ reads the outlet level. This is what makes $h_A = 280\ \mathrm{mm}$ and $h_D = 200\ \mathrm{mm}$ available as boundary conditions without any calculation.

The specimen is uniform, so $i$ is constant along it and the total head falls linearly with distance measured **along the axis**, not with elevation. The four points are equally spaced along the axis, so the head drops by equal amounts from one to the next.

The datum is the red dashed line at the bottom of the figure. Every elevation and every head in the solution is measured from it.

$\gamma_w = 9.81\ \mathrm{kN/m^3}$.

The statement asks for the heads "at point A and B", but the figure carries four tapping points and part 2 asks for all four standpipe levels. All four are worked below.

## Solution

The route is fixed by two boundary heads and one gradient: read $h$ at the two ends from the reservoir levels, get $i$ from their difference over the length of the specimen, then interpolate linearly to the interior points. Pressure head and pore pressure follow from $h_w = h - z$ and $u = h_w \gamma_w$, and the discharge from $q = kiA$.

1. Boundary total heads — the standpipes at the ends read their reservoirs, so $h_A = 280\ \mathrm{mm} = 28\ \mathrm{cm}$ and $h_D = 200\ \mathrm{mm} = 20\ \mathrm{cm}$
2. Hydraulic gradient — along the axis, $L_{AD} = 3 \times 60 = 180\ \mathrm{mm} = 18\ \mathrm{cm}$, so $i = \dfrac{h_A - h_D}{L_{AD}} = \dfrac{28 - 20}{18} = \mathbf{0.444}$ (dimensionless)
3. Total head at the interior points — equal spacing means an equal drop of $i \times 6 = 2.67\ \mathrm{cm}$ per interval: $h_B = 28 - 2.67 = 25.3\ \mathrm{cm}$ and $h_C = 25.3 - 2.67 = 22.7\ \mathrm{cm}$
4. Pressure heads — $h_w = h - z$ at each point
5. Pore pressures — $u = h_w \gamma_w$, with $h_w$ converted to metres

| Point | $z$ (cm) | $h$ (cm) | $h_w = h - z$ (cm) | $u = h_w\gamma_w$ (kPa) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 5.0 | 28.0 | 23.0 | 2.26 |
| B | 7.5 | 25.3 | 17.8 | 1.75 |
| C | 10.0 | 22.7 | 12.7 | 1.24 |
| D | 12.5 | 20.0 | 7.5 | 0.74 |

6. Standpipe levels — each standpipe rises to the total head of the point it taps, measured from the datum, so the four water surfaces sit at 28.0, 25.3, 22.7 and 20.0 cm above the datum and their tops fall on a straight line
7. Cross-sectional area — $A = \dfrac{\pi \phi^2}{4} = \dfrac{\pi (6)^2}{4} = 28.3\ \mathrm{cm^2}$
8. Discharge — $q = kiA = (3.4 \times 10^{-4})(0.444)(28.3) = \mathbf{4.27 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{cm^3/s}}$

Every value recomputed independently in Python. The geometry is internally consistent: the four points rise 25 mm in elevation for every 60 mm along the axis, which puts the specimen at $24.6^\circ$ to the horizontal, and that matches the inclination drawn in the SVG. The 80 mm dimension on the right of the figure is the difference between the two reservoir levels, $280 - 200$, which confirms that the total head loss across the specimen is 80 mm. Units check on every step: $h$, $h_w$ and $z$ are all lengths from the same datum, $i$ is dimensionless, and $u$ comes out in kPa only because $h_w$ was converted from cm to m before multiplying by $\gamma_w$ in $\mathrm{kN/m^3}$. Magnitude check: $k = 3.4 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{cm/s}$ is a silty sand, and the resulting trickle of a few thousandths of a cubic centimetre per second through a $28\ \mathrm{cm^2}$ face is what that permeability should give.

## Scaffolding decisions

Step 1 is blue on the two values only. Reading a reservoir level off a figure is not hard, but deciding that the standpipe at the end of the specimen reads that level is the assumption the whole solution rests on, so it gets a red note rather than a blank.

Step 2 is black on the equation, blue on $L_{AD}$ and on the substitution, green on $i$. $L_{AD}$ is deliberately blue: measuring the length **along the axis** rather than vertically is the trap in this example, and the student should have to commit to a number.

Step 3 is blue throughout. The equal-interval shortcut is pre-printed black so students see why the drop repeats.

The step 4–5 table is blanked by column: $z$ black, $h$ blue, $h_w$ blue, $u$ blue. Row A is worked in the steps above the table, so its cells can be pre-printed as the model row.

Step 6 is green on the description and, in the book version, on the four levels.

Step 8 is blue on the substitution and green on $q$.

Red notes: that the specimen is inclined so $L$ is measured along the axis while $z$ is measured vertically; that the standpipe reads total head and not pressure head, which is the single most common misreading of this figure; that pressure head must go to metres before meeting $\gamma_w$ in $\mathrm{kN/m^3}$; and that the tops of the four standpipes fall on a straight line only because the specimen is uniform.

The note on step 1 needs a `data-slide` short form. The full statement of the frictionless-pipework assumption runs past 200 characters and is worth keeping in the book.

## Flags

The statement says "at point A and B" but the figure has A, B, C and D and part 2 needs all four. Either the statement should be corrected to "at every point" or the solution should say why it computes four. The draft above does the latter, under Assumptions.

The legacy handwritten solution rounds $i$ to $0.44$ before interpolating and reports $h_B = 25.4\ \mathrm{cm}$ and $h_C = 22.6\ \mathrm{cm}$. Carrying $i = 0.4444$ gives $25.3$ and $22.7$. The difference is rounding only, but the new slide and the old SVG will disagree in the first decimal if anyone compares them.

The source figure `Example_tubes.svg` already draws the standpipe levels and the sloping head line, so it gives away part 2 before the student attempts it. Consider a student version of the figure with the standpipes empty.
