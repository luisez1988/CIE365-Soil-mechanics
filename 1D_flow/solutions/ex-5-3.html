Pick the right test equation The head is held constant, so Darcy's law applies unchanged for the whole test: \(V=q\,t=k\,i\,A\,t\), with \(i=\dfrac{h}{L}\). \(T=20^{\circ}\)C is the reference temperature for \(k\) — no viscosity correction. That is the only reason it is given.
Cross-sectional area \(A=\dfrac{\pi d^{2}}{4}=\) \(\dfrac{\pi(7.6)^{2}}{4}\) \(=\) 45.4 \(\unit{cm^2}\)
Volume collected \(V=\) 1054 \(\unit{cm^3}\) A mass was measured; Darcy's law needs a volume. At \(20^{\circ}\)C, \(\rho=1\unit{g/cm^3}\) — same number, different unit.
Flow rate \(q=\dfrac{V}{t}=\) \(\dfrac{1054}{120}\) \(=\) 8.78 \(\unit{cm^3/s}\) \(t=2\) min \(=120\) s. Convert before substituting.
Hydraulic gradient \(i=\dfrac{h}{L}=\) \(\dfrac{85}{17.8}\) \(=\) 4.78 Dimensionless: head lost per unit length of flow path. The denominator is \(L\), never the head.
Permeability \(k=\dfrac{q}{i\,A}=\) \(\dfrac{8.78}{4.78\times45.4}\) \(=\) \(4.05\times10^{-2}\) \(\unit{cm/s}\) Sanity check: a constant-head test only gives measurable flow in a fairly permeable soil, so expect the clean-sand range.
