## Statement

> A cylindrical soil sample, 7.6 cm in diameter and 17.8 cm long, is tested in a constant-head permeability apparatus. A constant head of 85 cm is maintained during the test. After 2 min of testing, a total of 1054 g of water was collected. The temperature was 20 °C. Calculate the permeability of the soil sample.

## Given / Find

- $d = 7.6\ \mathrm{cm}$, $L = 17.8\ \mathrm{cm}$, $h = 85\ \mathrm{cm}$
- $t = 2\ \mathrm{min}$, mass of water $= 1054\ \mathrm{g}$, $T = 20^\circ\mathrm{C}$
- **Find:** $k$

## Assumptions

None — the statement is complete. The temperature is given only to establish that no viscosity correction is needed ($20^\circ\mathrm{C}$ is the reference temperature at which $k$ is reported), which is worth saying out loud because students often go looking for a correction factor that is not required here.

## Solution

The head is held constant, so Darcy's law applies unchanged over the whole test: $V = q\,t = k\,i\,A\,t$ with $i = h/L$, hence $k = \dfrac{V L}{A h t}$.

1. Area — $A = \dfrac{\pi d^2}{4} = \dfrac{\pi (7.6)^2}{4} = 45.4\ \mathrm{cm^2}$
2. Volume — $1054\ \mathrm{g}$ of water at $20^\circ\mathrm{C}$ gives $V = 1054\ \mathrm{cm^3}$, since $\rho = 1\ \mathrm{g/cm^3}$
3. Flow rate — $q = \dfrac{V}{t} = \dfrac{1054}{120} = 8.78\ \mathrm{cm^3/s}$, with $t = 2\ \mathrm{min} = 120\ \mathrm{s}$
4. Gradient — $i = \dfrac{h}{L} = \dfrac{85}{17.8} = 4.78$ (dimensionless)
5. Permeability — $k = \dfrac{q}{i A} = \dfrac{8.78}{4.78 \times 45.4} = \mathbf{4.05 \times 10^{-2}\ \mathrm{cm/s}}$

## Verification

```
A   = pi*7.6^2/4        = 45.3646 cm2   -> 45.4
V   = 1054 g            = 1054 cm3
t   = 2 min             = 120 s
i   = h/L               = 4.775
q   = V/t               = 8.7833 cm3/s  -> 8.78
k   = V*L/(A*h*t)       = 0.040546 cm/s -> 4.055e-02
```

Every value recomputed independently in Python; nothing is transcribed. Cross-check with the *rounded* intermediates: $8.78/(4.78 \times 45.4) = 0.04046$, which rounds to the same $4.05 \times 10^{-2}$, so the rounding shown on the slide is self-consistent. Units check on every step. Answer reported to three significant figures, matching the precision of the given data.

## Scaffolding decisions

Step 1 carries no blanks at all — it is the route, and the student should see which equation applies and why before solving anything.

Blue covers both the substitution and its value on steps 1–5, so the student decides *which given goes where* rather than only doing arithmetic.

Green is $k$ alone.

Red notes: the reference temperature (why $T$ is given at all); mass to volume (what was measured is a mass, but the equation needs a volume); the $2\ \mathrm{min} \rightarrow 120\ \mathrm{s}$ conversion; and that $i$ is dimensionless with $L$ in the denominator — dividing by the head instead is the classic error here.

No `data-slide` short forms needed; every note fits inside the ~110-character slide budget.

## Flags

None. The result of order $10^{-2}\ \mathrm{cm/s}$ is a clean sand, which is consistent with a constant-head apparatus being the appropriate test for this soil — that sanity check is the closing red note.
