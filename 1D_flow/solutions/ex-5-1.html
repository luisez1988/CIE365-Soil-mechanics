Read the datum All elevations share one survey datum, so \(z\) comes straight off the figure: \(z_A=\) 1450 \(\unit{ft}\), water surface \(\text{WS}=\) 1490 \(\unit{ft}\) Pick a datum once and keep it. Any other choice shifts every \(z\) and every \(h\) by the same constant, and leaves every head difference untouched.
Pressure head at A \(\hp=\text{WS}-z_A=\) \(1490-1450\) \(=\) 40 \(\unit{ft}\) \(\hp\) is the height of water standing above the point, not its depth below the ground surface.
Total head at A \(h=z+\hp=\) \(1450+40\) \(=\) 1490 \(\unit{ft}\) Two lines, and they are the only two. The rest of the table is the same pair at a different elevation.

Piezometric line It is the plot of \(h\) against position, so here a horizontal line at elevation 1490 \(\unit{ft}\) — it lands on the reservoir surface.
Is there flow? \(\Delta h=0\) between every pair, so \(i=\dfrac{\Delta h}{L}=\) 0 and the water does not move. Four points, three elevations, one total head. That is what hydrostatics requires, and it is the check.
