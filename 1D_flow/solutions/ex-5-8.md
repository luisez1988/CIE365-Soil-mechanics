## Statement

> A constant head permeability test was conducted on a chamber of 6 inches of diameter by 12 inches heigh. The soil was placed between two porous stones of 1/2 inches of diameter with a permeability of $k = 1 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{cm/s}$ each. The test lasted 1.5 hr and 2 lt. of water were collected. The total head difference was 1 m. Find the permeability of the sand considering the effect of the porous stones.

## Given / Find

- Chamber: 6 in diameter by 12 in height
- Two porous stones, 1/2 in, $k_{stone} = 1 \times 10^{-3}\ \mathrm{cm/s}$ each
- $t = 1.5\ \mathrm{hr}$, $V = 2\ \mathrm{L}$, $\Delta h = 1\ \mathrm{m}$
- **Find:** $k$ of the sand, corrected for the stones

## Assumptions

**Two readings of the statement have to be pinned down, and both change the answer.**

The stones' "1/2 inches" is taken as **thickness, not diameter**: a 1/2 in diameter stone cannot seal against a 6 in chamber wall, so the stones must span the full bore.

The 12 in chamber height is taken as **the whole stack**, so soil and both stones fit inside it and $L_{soil} = 12'' - 2(\tfrac{1}{2}'') = 11'' = 27.94\ \mathrm{cm}$.

Both are stated on the slide as a red note, because "state your assumptions before you compute" is itself the lesson here. **Confirm these are what you intended** — if the 12 in refers to the soil alone, $L_{soil}$ becomes $30.48\ \mathrm{cm}$ and the answer moves.

## Solution

Water crosses three materials in a row: stone, soil, stone. They are in **series** — each carries the same $q$ but loses a different share of the head — so resistances add: $\dfrac{L_{tot}}{k_{eq}} = \sum_i \dfrac{L_i}{k_i}$. Defining $R = L/k$ (units of seconds) turns the problem into arithmetic: $R_{tot} = 2R_{stone} + R_{soil}$.

1. Geometry in consistent units — $D = 15.24\ \mathrm{cm}$, $L_{tot} = 30.48\ \mathrm{cm}$, $t_{stone} = 1.27\ \mathrm{cm}$, so $L_{soil} = 30.48 - 2(1.27) = 27.94\ \mathrm{cm}$
2. Area — $A = \dfrac{\pi D^2}{4} = \dfrac{\pi (15.24)^2}{4} = 182.4\ \mathrm{cm^2}$
3. What the test actually measures — the whole stack, so it returns $k_{eq}$, not the soil: $k_{eq} = \dfrac{V L_{tot}}{A\,\Delta h\,t} = \dfrac{2000 \times 30.48}{182.4 \times 100 \times 5400} = 6.19 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{cm/s}$
4. Total resistance measured — $R_{tot} = \dfrac{L_{tot}}{k_{eq}} = \dfrac{30.48}{6.19 \times 10^{-4}} = 49\,250\ \mathrm{s}$
5. Resistance the stones are responsible for — $2R_{stone} = \dfrac{2\,t_{stone}}{k_{stone}} = \dfrac{2(1.27)}{1 \times 10^{-3}} = 2\,540\ \mathrm{s}$
6. Permeability of the sand — $k_{soil} = \dfrac{L_{soil}}{R_{tot} - 2R_{stone}} = \dfrac{27.94}{49\,250 - 2\,540} = \mathbf{5.98 \times 10^{-4}\ \mathrm{cm/s}}$

## Verification

```
A      = pi*(15.24)^2/4  = 182.415 cm2 -> 182.4
Ltot   = 12 in           = 30.48 cm ; stone 1.27 cm ; Lsoil = 27.94 cm
k_eq   = Q*Ltot/(A h t)  = 6.188585e-04 cm/s   (ignoring stones)
R_tot  = Ltot/keq        = 49252.0 s
R_st   = 2*t/k_st        = 2540.0 s   (5.2% of total)
k_soil = Lsoil/(Rtot-Rst)= 5.981337e-04 cm/s -> 5.981e-04
err if stones ignored    = +3.5%
```

Every value recomputed independently in Python. Unit conversions used: $V = 2\ \mathrm{L} = 2000\ \mathrm{cm^3}$, $\Delta h = 1\ \mathrm{m} = 100\ \mathrm{cm}$, $t = 1.5\ \mathrm{hr} = 5400\ \mathrm{s}$, $1'' = 2.54\ \mathrm{cm}$. Sanity check: the correction moves the answer the *right way* — accounting for the stones' resistance lowers the sand's inferred $k$, because some of the measured head loss belongs to the stones rather than to the soil.

## Flags

The two assumptions above are the main thing to check. Both are defensible readings of an ambiguous statement, and both move the number.

Consider whether the statement itself should be corrected in the deck to say "1/2 in thick" — that would remove the ambiguity, though the assumption-stating exercise has teaching value of its own.
