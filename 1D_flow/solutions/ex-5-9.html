See the system for what it is The water crosses the three sands side by side. Layers in parallel share \(\Delta h\) and \(L\), so their discharges add: \(\keq=\dfrac{\sum k_iA_i}{A}\). Parallel layers share the head difference and split the discharge; series layers share the discharge and split the head. That fork is the whole decision here, and it is the opposite of the stone-and-soil stack in Example 5.8.
Head difference \(\Delta h=\) \(137.23-125.5\) \(=\) 11.73 \(\unit{ft}\) Four elevations appear on the figure: two are water surfaces and two are the base of the sand package. Only the water surfaces set \(\Delta h\).
Flow path, then gradient The layers drop \(128.5-115.5=13\unit{ft}\) over \(1255.6\unit{ft}\), so \(L=\sqrt{1255.6^{2}+13^{2}}=\) 1255.7 \(\unit{ft}\) and \(i=\dfrac{\Delta h}{L}=\) \(\dfrac{11.73}{1255.7}\) \(=\) \(\ee{9.34}{-3}\) The path follows the layers, not the horizontal. Here that is worth 0.005% — but say it.
Equivalent permeability Unit width, so areas go as thicknesses: \(\keq=\dfrac{\sum k_ib_i}{\sum b_i}=\) \(\dfrac{(\ee{1.5}{-5})(3)+(\ee{7.89}{-3})(3)+(\ee{1.2}{-4})(4)}{10}\) \(=\) \(\ee{2.42}{-3}\) \(\unit{cm/s}\) A thickness-weighted average, so the most permeable layer dominates — the SC contributes 0.2% of the total and could be dropped without moving the answer. In the series case the least permeable layer runs the result instead. That is the argument for spending the investigation budget on the SP.
Flow area, per unit length of channel The full package carries the flow: \(A=10\unit{ft}\times1=\) 304.8 \(\unit{cm}\) of face per cm of channel \(\keq\) is in cm/s, so the area must be in cm before the two meet.
Seepage per unit length of channel \(q=\keq\,i\,A=\) \((\ee{2.42}{-3})(\ee{9.34}{-3})(304.8)\) \(=\) \(\ee{6.89}{-3}\) \(\unit{cm^3/s}\) per cm \(=\) \(\ee{6.89}{-4}\) \(\unit{L/s}\) per m \(=\) 59.5 \(\unit{L/day}\) per m Going from \(\unit{cm^3/s}\) per cm to \(\unit{L/s}\) per m looks like a typo but is not: the volume shrinks by 1000 while the length grows by 100, so the net factor is \(\times0.1\) and only the exponent moves. Then \(\times86\,400\) for the day. The answer is per unit length along the channel, so multiply by the length of channel in this ground to get the loss the scheme has to carry.
