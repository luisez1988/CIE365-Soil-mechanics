See the system for what it is Water crosses three materials in a row: stone, soil, stone. They are in series, so each carries the same \(q\) but loses a different share of the head. Resistances add: \(\dfrac{L_{tot}}{k_{eq}}=\displaystyle\sum_i\dfrac{L_i}{k_i}\). Define \(R=L/k\) (units of s) and the problem becomes arithmetic: \(R_{tot}=2R_{stone}+R_{soil}\). Only series layers add this way — parallel layers add \(k_iL_i\) instead.
Geometry, in consistent units \(D=6''=\) 15.24 cm,   \(L_{tot}=12''=\) 30.48 cm,   \(t_{stone}=\tfrac12''=\) 1.27 cm
\(L_{soil}=L_{tot}-2\,t_{stone}=\) \(30.48-2(1.27)\) \(=\) 27.94 cm Two readings of the statement have to be pinned down. A stone "of 1/2 inch" must be its thickness — it has to span the full 6″ chamber to seal against the wall. And the 12″ chamber height is taken as the whole stack, so the soil is what is left after both stones. State assumptions like these before you compute; they change the answer.
Cross-sectional area \(A=\dfrac{\pi D^{2}}{4}=\) \(\dfrac{\pi(15.24)^{2}}{4}\) \(=\) 182.4 \(\unit{cm^2}\)
What the test actually measures The test sees the whole stack, so it returns \(k_{eq}\), not the soil: \(k_{eq}=\dfrac{V\,L_{tot}}{A\,\Delta h\,t}=\) \(\dfrac{2000\times30.48}{182.4\times100\times5400}\) \(=\) \(6.19\times10^{-4}\) \(\unit{cm/s}\) \(V=2\unit{L}=2000\unit{cm^3}\), \(\Delta h=1\unit{m}=100\unit{cm}\), \(t=1.5\unit{hr}=5400\unit{s}\). This is the number you would report if you forgot the stones — hold on to it for comparison.
Total resistance measured \(R_{tot}=\dfrac{L_{tot}}{k_{eq}}=\) \(\dfrac{30.48}{6.19\times10^{-4}}\) \(=\) 49\,250 \(\unit{s}\)
Resistance the stones are responsible for \(2R_{stone}=\dfrac{2\,t_{stone}}{k_{stone}}=\) \(\dfrac{2(1.27)}{1\times10^{-3}}\) \(=\) 2\,540 \(\unit{s}\) The stones are far thinner than the soil but only ten times more permeable, so they are not negligible — thickness and permeability both matter, only the ratio \(L/k\) counts.
Permeability of the sand \(k_{soil}=\dfrac{L_{soil}}{R_{tot}-2R_{stone}}=\) \(\dfrac{27.94}{49\,250-2\,540}\) \(=\) \(5.98\times10^{-4}\) \(\unit{cm/s}\)
Worth the effort? The stones turn out to carry 5.2% of the total resistance, so ignoring them overestimates the sand's permeability by about 3.5%. Small here — but the error grows quickly as the soil becomes more permeable relative to the stones, which is exactly when you are most tempted to skip the correction.
