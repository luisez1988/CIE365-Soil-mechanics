## Statement

> The void ratio of a clay is 0.5 and the degree of saturation is 70%. Assuming the density of solids is 2750 kg/m<sup>3</sup>, calculate: 1. The water content 2. The dry unit weight 3. The total unit weight

Self-contained. Note what is *not* given: no mass and no volume anywhere, only ratios and a density — which is the signal that the sample can be scaled freely.

## Given / Find

- $e = 0.5$, $S = 70\% = 0.70$, $\rho_s = 2750\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$
- **Find:** $w$, $\gamma_d$, $\gamma_t$

## Assumptions

**$\rho_w = 1000\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$ and $g = 9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$.** Neither is given. Both are licensed by the deck's "useful steps" slide; $\rho_w$ is needed to turn $\rho_s$ into $G_s$, and $g$ is needed because the answers are asked for as *unit weights* while the data is a *density*.

**The sample is scaled to $V_s = 1\ \mathrm{m^3}$.** No mass or volume is given, so this is legal — rule 4 of the deck's procedure slide. Every requested quantity is a ratio and is therefore unaffected by the choice.

**Air is weightless**, so the $30\%$ of the voids that are not water contribute volume to $V_t$ but nothing to $W_t$.

## Solution

This is Example 2.2 run forwards with numbers: the identity $Se = wG_s$ gives the water content directly, and the phase-diagram result $\gamma_t = \frac{(G_s + Se)\gamma_w}{1+e}$ gives the unit weights. The solution below builds the diagram explicitly rather than quoting the formulas, because the point of the example is the bookkeeping, but every step is cross-checked against the closed form in the Verification section.

1. Specific gravity — $G_s = \dfrac{\rho_s}{\rho_w} = \dfrac{2750}{1000} = 2.75$
2. Water content — from $Se = wG_s$, $w = \dfrac{Se}{G_s} = \dfrac{0.70 \times 0.5}{2.75} = \dfrac{0.35}{2.75} = 0.1273 = \mathbf{12.7\%}$
3. Dry density — with $V_s = 1$, $V_t = 1 + e = 1.5\ \mathrm{m^3}$ and $m_s = \rho_s V_s = 2750\ \mathrm{kg}$, so $\rho_d = \dfrac{m_s}{V_t} = \dfrac{2750}{1.5} = 1833\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$
4. Dry unit weight — $\gamma_d = \rho_d\, g = 1833 \times 9.81 = 17985\ \mathrm{N/m^3} = \mathbf{18.0\ \mathrm{kN/m^3}}$
5. Total density — the water adds weight but no volume, since it sits in voids that are already counted: $\rho_t = \rho_d(1 + w) = 1833 \times 1.1273 = 2067\ \mathrm{kg/m^3}$
6. Total unit weight — $\gamma_t = \rho_t\, g = 2067 \times 9.81 = 20274\ \mathrm{N/m^3} = \mathbf{20.3\ \mathrm{kN/m^3}}$
7. Validate — against the deck's typical-values table, $\gamma_d = 18.0\ \mathrm{kN/m^3}$ sits at the very top of the silt-and-clay band (6–18 kN/m³); this is a dense clay, which is what $e = 0.5$ says

Each answer was recomputed independently. Note that $\rho_t$ in the script is computed from the closed form $\frac{(G_s + Se)}{1+e}\rho_w$, whereas step 5 of the solution computes it as $\rho_d(1+w)$; the two agree at 2066.67 kg/m³, which is a genuine independent check of the route rather than a restatement. Likewise $\gamma_d$ from $\rho_s/(1+e) \times g$ agrees with $G_s\gamma_w/(1+e)$ to 17.98 versus 17.99 kN/m³ — the 0.01 gap is only because $\gamma_w$ was taken as the rounded 9.81 kN/m³ in the second route rather than $1000 \times 9.81$ N/m³. Units: densities in kg/m³ times $g$ in m/s² give N/m³, divided by 1000 for kN/m³; $w$, $e$, $S$ and $G_s$ are dimensionless. Magnitude check: $\gamma_t = 20.3\ \mathrm{kN/m^3}$ is heavy but plausible for a stiff clay, and the saturated value the same soil would reach, 21.3 kN/m³, is just outside the table's silt-and-clay band — see Flags.

## Scaffolding decisions

Step 1 is one division and would be trivial as a blank, but it is kept blue because converting a *density* into the dimensionless $G_s$ is the move that makes everything downstream work, and students who leave $\rho_s$ in kg/m³ carry the units into $Se = wG_s$ and produce nonsense.

Step 2 pre-prints $Se = wG_s$ in black — it was proved in Example 2.2, so recalling it is not the exercise; rearranging it for $w$ and substituting is. The blank is on $\frac{0.70 \times 0.5}{2.75}$, so the student must place $G_s$ in the denominator.

Step 3 is the one step that needs the explicit phase diagram, so it gets the most black scaffolding: $V_s = 1$, $V_t = 1 + e$, and $m_s = \rho_s V_s$ are all pre-printed as a small HTML table, with only $V_t = 1.5$, $m_s = 2750$ and the resulting $\rho_d$ in blue. The alternative — handing over $\rho_d = \rho_s/(1+e)$ as a formula — would hide exactly the reasoning this chapter exists to teach.

Steps 4 and 6 are deliberately separate steps from 3 and 5 even though each is a single multiplication by $g$. Density and unit weight are different physical quantities, and the problem asked for unit weights while giving a density; collapsing the conversion into the preceding step is how students end up reporting kg/m³ as an answer. Both $\gamma_d$ and $\gamma_t$ are green.

Step 5's factor $(1 + w)$ is blue but the reasoning behind it is black: water adds weight without adding volume. That sentence is the whole content of the step.

Step 7 is entirely black except for the band it is compared against — validation is reading, not deciding.

Red notes, at most one per step: (1) $G_s$ is dimensionless; dividing by $\rho_w$ is what strips the units; (3) legal to set $V_s = 1$ only because no mass or volume is given — rule 4 from the procedure slide; (4) density $\times\, g$ = unit weight, so an answer in kg/m³ is not a unit weight; (5) adding water at constant $e$ changes $W_t$ but not $V_t$.

Note (3) repeats the same idea as the corresponding note in Example 2.2. That repetition is intentional — 2.2 is a derivation and 2.4 is the first place a student has to *act* on the rule — but if the two examples are ever shown back to back, the 2.4 wording should be shortened to a `data-slide` form such as "No mass or volume given, so $V_s = 1$ is free."

## Flags

The result sits at the edge of the deck's own validation table, which is worth the author's attention. $e = 0.5$ is low for a clay, and it drives $\gamma_d = 18.0\ \mathrm{kN/m^3}$ to the exact top of the table's silt-and-clay range (6–18), while the saturated unit weight this soil would have, $\gamma_{sat} = 21.3\ \mathrm{kN/m^3}$, is marginally *above* the tabulated range (14–21). The numbers are internally consistent and the arithmetic is verified; the issue is only that step 7 asks students to validate against a table this soil barely fits. Options: describe the soil as a stiff or overconsolidated clay in the statement, soften step 7 to note that a dense clay lands at the top of the band, or change $e$ to something like 0.7. My recommendation is to keep the numbers and let step 7 make the point explicitly — a result at the edge of a range is a more instructive validation than one in the middle, provided the note says so.
