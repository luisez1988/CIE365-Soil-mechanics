## Statement

> In the natural state, a moist soil has a volume of 0.0093 m<sup>3</sup> and weighs 177.6 N. The oven dry weigth of the soil is 153.6 N. If $G_s = 2.71$, calculate: 1. Gravimetric water content. 2. Total unit weight. 3. Void ratio and porosity. 4. Saturation ratio.

Self-contained: total volume, total weight, dry weight and $G_s$ are all in the text, and that is exactly enough to fill the phase diagram completely.

## Given / Find

- $V_t = 0.0093\ \mathrm{m^3}$, $W_t = 177.6\ \mathrm{N}$, $W_s = 153.6\ \mathrm{N}$ (oven-dry), $G_s = 2.71$
- **Find:** $w$, $\gamma_t$, $e$ and $n$, and $S$

## Assumptions

**$\gamma_w = 9.81\ \mathrm{kN/m^3}$.** The statement does not give it. This is the value the deck's own "useful steps" slide licenses, and it is needed twice — once to convert $W_s$ into $V_s$ and once to convert $W_w$ into $V_w$.

**Oven-drying removes all the water and none of the solids.** So the 153.6 N is $W_s$ exactly, and the difference $177.6 - 153.6$ is entirely water. This is the standard reading of "oven dry weight" and is what makes the problem solvable.

**The soil is not saturated.** The statement calls it "moist", and the answer $S = 69.5\%$ confirms it — the remaining $30.5\%$ of the void volume is air, which carries no weight.

## Solution

Nothing here needs a formula beyond the definitions: the four unknowns are all ratios of elementary volumes and weights, and the statement gives enough of both columns of the phase diagram to fill it. The one non-obvious move is the "cross-over" — $G_s\gamma_w$ is the only bridge from the weight column to the volume column, and it is used in steps 4 and 7.

1. Weight of water — $W_w = W_t - W_s = 177.6 - 153.6 = 24.0\ \mathrm{N}$
2. Gravimetric water content — $w = \dfrac{W_w}{W_s} = \dfrac{24.0}{153.6} = 0.1563 = \mathbf{15.6\%}$
3. Total unit weight — $\gamma_t = \dfrac{W_t}{V_t} = \dfrac{177.6}{0.0093} = 19097\ \mathrm{N/m^3} = \mathbf{19.10\ \mathrm{kN/m^3}}$
4. Volume of solids (cross over) — $V_s = \dfrac{W_s}{G_s\gamma_w} = \dfrac{153.6}{2.71 \times 9810} = 0.005778\ \mathrm{m^3}$
5. Volume of voids — $V_v = V_t - V_s = 0.0093 - 0.005778 = 0.003522\ \mathrm{m^3}$
6. Void ratio and porosity — $e = \dfrac{V_v}{V_s} = \dfrac{0.003522}{0.005778} = \mathbf{0.610}$ and $n = \dfrac{e}{1+e} = \dfrac{0.610}{1.610} = \mathbf{0.379} = \mathbf{37.9\%}$
7. Volume of water (cross over again) — $V_w = \dfrac{W_w}{\gamma_w} = \dfrac{24.0}{9810} = 0.002446\ \mathrm{m^3}$
8. Saturation ratio — $S = \dfrac{V_w}{V_v} = \dfrac{0.002446}{0.003522} = 0.695 = \mathbf{69.5\%}$
9. Validate — $Se = 0.695 \times 0.610 = 0.4234$ and $wG_s = 0.1563 \times 2.71 = 0.4234$, so the identity proved in Example 2.2 holds; and $\gamma_d = W_s/V_t = 16.52\ \mathrm{kN/m^3}$ agrees with both $\gamma_t/(1+w)$ and $G_s\gamma_w/(1+e)$

Every answer was recomputed independently from the given data, and each of the four requested quantities was then cross-checked by a route that does not reuse the step that produced it: $n$ against $V_v/V_t$ (0.3787 both ways), $S$ against the identity $Se = wG_s$ (exact to the last digit printed), and $\gamma_t$ against the closed form $\frac{(G_s+Se)\gamma_w}{1+e}$ derived in Example 2.2. The dry unit weight, which the problem does not ask for, was computed three independent ways as a further check and gives 16.52 kN/m³ each time. Units: $W/V$ gives N/m³ throughout, divided by 1000 for kN/m³; $w$, $e$, $n$ and $S$ are all dimensionless. Magnitude check against the deck's typical-values table: $\gamma_d = 16.5\ \mathrm{kN/m^3}$ and $e = 0.61$ sit comfortably inside the sand/gravel band (15–23 kN/m³) and at the upper end of the silt/clay band, which is consistent with an unremarkable natural moist soil.

## Scaffolding decisions

Steps 1 and 2 are the cheapest in the problem and the most commonly botched, so both get blue blanks on the substitution. The classic error is dividing by $W_t$ rather than $W_s$, and blanking only the final value would not catch it — the blank is on the fraction $\frac{24.0}{153.6}$, so the student has to commit to a denominator.

Step 4 is the pivot of the whole solution and gets the longest black lead-in: the symbolic form $V_s = W_s/(G_s\gamma_w)$ is pre-printed, and only the substitution and result are blue. Pre-printing the symbolic form is deliberate — the decision being tested here is *recognising that a cross-over is needed*, not recalling the formula, and the black text makes the route visible while the blank still requires the arithmetic.

Step 6 produces two quantities but one physical fact — how much void there is — so it stays one step. Both $e$ and $n$ are green; both were asked for.

Step 7 mirrors step 4 with $G_s$ absent, which is the point: water has $G = 1$. That contrast is carried by a red note rather than by extra algebra.

Step 9 is pre-printed black with the two products in blue. It is the "validate" step from the deck's own procedure slide, and making the student compute both sides of $Se = wG_s$ is the cheapest possible reinforcement of Example 2.2.

Red notes, at most one per step: (2) $w$ divides by the weight of *solids*, never the total — which is why $w$ can legitimately exceed 100%; (4) $G_s\gamma_w$ is the unit weight of the grains, and this is the only bridge between the two columns of the diagram; (5) keep $V_s$ to at least four significant figures, because $V_v$ is a small difference of two similar numbers; (7) dividing by $\gamma_w$ alone, not $G_s\gamma_w$, because water's specific gravity is 1.

The step-5 note about significant figures is worth keeping even though it looks fussy: rounding $V_s$ to 0.0058 shifts $e$ from 0.610 to 0.603 and $S$ from 69.5% to 70.5%, which is enough to make a student think they have made an error elsewhere.

None of the notes exceed the length budget, so no `data-slide` short forms are anticipated.
