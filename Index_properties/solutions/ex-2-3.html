Weight of water \(W_w=W_t-W_s=\) \(177.6-153.6\) \(=\) 24.0 \(\unit{N}\) "Oven dry weight" is the weight of the solids because all the water is gone
Gravimetric water content \(w=\dfrac{W_w}{W_s}=\) \(\dfrac{24.0}{153.6}\) \(=\) 15.6 % Divide by the weight of solids, never the total weight, which is why \(w> 100\%\)
Total unit weight \(\gamma_t=\dfrac{W_t}{V_t}=\) \(\dfrac{177.6}{0.0093}\) \(=\) \(19\,097\) \(\unit{N/m^3}=\) 19.10 \(\unit{kN/m^3}\)
Cross over to volumes \(V_s=\dfrac{W_s}{\Gs\gw}=\) \(\dfrac{153.6}{2.71\times9810}\) \(=\) 0.005778 \(\unit{m^3}\)
Volume of voids \(V_v=V_t-V_s=\) \(0.0093-0.005778\) \(=\) 0.003522 \(\unit{m^3}\) Keep \(V_s\) to four significant figures: \(V_v\) is a small difference of two similar numbers.
Void ratio and porosity \(e=\dfrac{V_v}{V_s}=\) \(\dfrac{0.003522}{0.005778}\) \(=\) 0.610
\(n=\dfrac{e}{1+e}=\) \(\dfrac{0.610}{1.610}\) \(=\) 37.9 % Same \(V_v\) in both, only the denominator changes, \(V_s\) for \(e\) and \(V_t\) for \(n\).
Volume of water \(V_w=\dfrac{W_w}{\gw}=\) \(\dfrac{24.0}{9810}\) \(=\) 0.002446 \(\unit{m^3}\)
Saturation ratio \(S=\dfrac{V_w}{V_v}=\) \(\dfrac{0.002446}{0.003522}\) \(=\) 69.5 %
Validate The identity proved in Example 2.2 has to hold on real data: \(Se=\) \(0.695\times0.610 =0.4234\)  and  \(w\Gs=\) \(0.1563\times2.71 =0.4234\). ✓ A check worth doing!
