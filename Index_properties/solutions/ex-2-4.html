Specific gravity of the solids \(\Gs=\dfrac{\rho_s}{\rho_w}=\) \(\dfrac{2750}{1000}\) \(=\) 2.75 \(\Gs\) is dimensionless — dividing by \(\rho_w\) is what strips the units off \(\rho_s\).
Water content From \(Se=w\Gs\) (Example 2.2),  \(w=\dfrac{Se}{\Gs}=\) \(\dfrac{0.70\times0.5}{2.75}\) \(=\) 12.7 % \(\Gs\) goes in the denominator. On top it still returns a plausible-looking number.
Dry density
TakeThenValue
\(V_s=1\ \unit{m^3}\) \(V_t=1+e\) \(=\) 1.5 \(\unit{m^3}\)
\(\rho_s=2750\) \(m_s=\rho_s V_s\) \(=\) 2750 \(\unit{kg}\)
\(\rho_d=\dfrac{m_s}{V_t}=\) \(\dfrac{2750}{1.5}\) \(=\) 1833 \(\unit{kg/m^3}\) Setting \(V_s=1\) is free here: no mass or volume is given, so no answer can depend on it.
Dry unit weight \(\gd=\rho_d\,g=\) \(1833\times9.81\) \(=\) \(17\,985\) \(\unit{N/m^3}=\) 18.0 \(\unit{kN/m^3}\) Density \(\times\,g\) = unit weight. An answer left in \(\unit{kg/m^3}\) is not a unit weight.
Total density \(\rho_t=\rho_d(1+w)=\) \(1833\times1.1273\) \(=\) 2067 \(\unit{kg/m^3}\) Water adds weight but no volume — it sits in voids that \(V_t\) already counts, so only the numerator grows.
Total unit weight \(\gamma_t=\rho_t\,g=\) \(2067\times9.81\) \(=\) \(20\,274\) \(\unit{N/m^3}=\) 20.3 \(\unit{kN/m^3}\)
Validate Both unit weights land at the dense end of the silt-and-clay band in the typical-values table — which is what \(e=0.5\) describes. Validated. Those bands are indicative, not limits: landing at the edge of one is a pass, not a red flag.
