## Statement

> Given the sieve analysis and plasticity data for the following three soils, classify each soil according to the USCS and provide a group name.

Self-contained apart from one gap: the table names its sieves by number and the deck prints no openings anywhere, which blocks Soil 2's gradation coefficients. See Assumptions and Flags — this is the same gap that was closed in `grain_size` by adding the opening to each header cell.

## Given / Find

The slide's table, percent finer:

| Sieve | Soil 1 | Soil 2 | Soil 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| No. 4 | 99 | 97 | 100 |
| No. 10 | 92 | 90 | 100 |
| No. 40 | 86 | 40 | 100 |
| No. 100 | 78 | 8 | 99 |
| No. 200 | 60 | 5 | 97 |
| $LL$ (%) | 20 | — | 124 |
| $PL$ (%) | 15 | — | 47 |
| $PI$ (%) | 5 | NP | 77 |

- **Find:** for each soil, the USCS group symbol and the group name

## Assumptions

**All three soils are inorganic.** The plasticity chart labels its cells "CL or OL" and "CH or OH", and separating the two requires the oven-dried liquid limit ratio — ASTM D2487 calls a soil organic when $LL_{\mathrm{oven\text{-}dried}}/LL_{\mathrm{not\ dried}} < 0.75$ — which is not given for any of the three. Taken as inorganic throughout, so Soil 1 is CL-ML rather than OL and Soil 3 is CH rather than OH. This matters most for Soil 3: $LL = 124\ \%$ with $PI = 77\ \%$ is precisely the corner of the chart where a highly organic clay would be the competing reading, and the statement gives nothing to rule it out.

**The sieve numbers are the ASTM E11 openings** — No. 4 $= 4.75$, No. 10 $= 2.00$, No. 40 $= 0.425$, No. 100 $= 0.150$, No. 200 $= 0.075\ \mathrm{mm}$. This is not cosmetic: Soil 2 needs $D_{60}$, $D_{30}$ and $D_{10}$, and without the openings there is no horizontal axis to plot against and no gradation coefficients at all. The same five values were verified against `grain_size`'s own `GSD_plot.svg` in `ex-2-7.md`, so they are the openings this course's figures are drawn with.

**The table is percent *passing*, i.e. percent finer.** Its header says so, and Soil 3's $100\ \%$ at the No. 4 confirms the direction. Recorded because every fraction in step 1 is read straight off this column and the deck's own GSD-reading slide warns the axis is published both ways.

**Soil 2's $D_x$ values are read off the grain size distribution the student plots**, and reported to three significant figures — the convention set in Example 2.6 for this course. They are confirmed to the digit by logarithmic interpolation on the sieve table in the Verification section, so no second set of numbers ever reaches the slide.

**"NP" means the fines are non-plastic and therefore silt.** For a coarse-grained soil needing a dual symbol, the fines are classified on the plasticity chart like any fine-grained soil; a non-plastic result is ML, so the fines letter is M, not "no letter". This is a reading of the table rather than a choice, but it is the step students most often skip.

## Solution

Every soil follows the same three questions in the same order — what are the fractions, is it coarse or fine, and what do the fines do — and the whole classification hangs on the first answer, because $F_F$ alone decides whether the plasticity chart or the gradation coefficients are the relevant tool. The group *name* is then a separate lookup driven by thresholds at $15\ \%$ and $30\ \%$ of coarse material, which is where most of the marks are lost.

**Soil 1** (steps 1–4)

1. Soil fractions — gravel is what the No. 4 retains, $G_F = 100 - 99 = 1\ \%$; fines are what the No. 200 passes, $F_F = 60\ \%$; sand is the remainder, $S_F = 100 - 60 - 1 = 39\ \%$
2. Coarse or fine? — $F_F = 60\ \% \geq 50\ \%$, so the soil is **fine-grained** and the plasticity chart applies; the sieve curve plays no further part
3. Group symbol — $LL = 20\ \% < 50\ \%$ so the soil is low plasticity (L); the A-line is $PI_A = 0.73(LL - 20) = 0.73(20 - 20) = 0$, and $PI = 5 \geq 0$ puts the point **above** the A-line, so the fines are clay-like; but $4 \leq PI = 5 \leq 7$ places it in the hatched band, giving the dual symbol $\mathbf{CL\text{-}ML}$
4. Group name — coarse material is $G_F + S_F = 1 + 39 = 40\ \% \geq 30\ \%$, so a secondary descriptor is required; $S_F = 39\ \% > G_F = 1\ \%$ makes it "sandy"; and $G_F = 1\ \% < 15\ \%$ means no tertiary descriptor, so the soil is **CL-ML, sandy silty clay**

**Soil 2** (steps 5–11)

5. Soil fractions — $G_F = 100 - 97 = 3\ \%$, $F_F = 5\ \%$, $S_F = 100 - 5 - 3 = 92\ \%$
6. Coarse or fine, and which coarse? — $F_F = 5\ \% < 50\ \%$, so the soil is **coarse-grained** and the gradation coefficients apply; $S_F = 92\ \% > G_F = 3\ \%$, so the first letter is $\mathbf{S}$
7. Does it need a dual symbol? — $F_F = 5\ \%$ falls in the band $5\ \% \leq F_F \leq 12\ \%$, where neither the gradation alone nor the fines alone describe the soil, so **both** a gradation letter and a fines letter are required
8. Sizes from the GSD — plotting the five points and reading at $60\ \%$, $30\ \%$ and $10\ \%$ finer gives $D_{60} = 0.790\ \mathrm{mm}$, $D_{30} = 0.307\ \mathrm{mm}$, $D_{10} = 0.160\ \mathrm{mm}$
9. Gradation coefficients — $C_u = \dfrac{D_{60}}{D_{10}} = \dfrac{0.790}{0.160} = 4.93$ and $C_c = \dfrac{D_{30}^2}{D_{10}D_{60}} = \dfrac{(0.307)^2}{0.160 \times 0.790} = 0.745$; a well-graded sand needs $C_u \geq 6$ **and** $1 \leq C_c \leq 3$, and both fail, so the gradation letter is $\mathbf{P}$
10. Fines letter and dual symbol — the fines are non-plastic, so they classify as ML and the fines letter is $\mathbf{M}$, giving $\mathbf{SP\text{-}SM}$
11. Group name — $G_F = 3\ \% < 15\ \%$, so no gravel is named, and the soil is **SP-SM, poorly graded sand with silt**

**Soil 3** (steps 12–15)

12. Soil fractions — $G_F = 100 - 100 = 0\ \%$, $F_F = 97\ \%$, $S_F = 100 - 97 - 0 = 3\ \%$
13. Coarse or fine? — $F_F = 97\ \% \geq 50\ \%$, so the soil is **fine-grained** and the plasticity chart applies
14. Group symbol — $LL = 124\ \% \geq 50\ \%$ so the soil is high plasticity (H); the A-line gives $PI_A = 0.73(124 - 20) = 75.9$, and $PI = 77 > 75.9$ puts the point **above** the A-line, so the soil is $\mathbf{CH}$; the U-line check $PI_U = 0.9(124 - 8) = 104$ confirms $PI = 77 < 104$, so the limits are physically plausible and no test error is indicated
15. Group name — coarse material is $G_F + S_F = 0 + 3 = 3\ \% < 15\ \%$, so no descriptor of any kind is added, and the soil is **CH, fat clay**

## Verification

```python
import math

op = {"#4": 4.75, "#10": 2.00, "#40": 0.425, "#100": 0.150, "#200": 0.075}
sv = ["#4", "#10", "#40", "#100", "#200"]
PP = {1: [99, 92, 86, 78, 60], 2: [97, 90, 40, 8, 5], 3: [100, 100, 100, 99, 97]}
LL = {1: 20, 3: 124}; PL = {1: 15, 3: 47}; PI = {1: 5, 3: 77}

Aline = lambda L: 0.73 * (L - 20)
Uline = lambda L: 0.90 * (L - 8)

frac = {}
for s in (1, 2, 3):
    p = dict(zip(sv, PP[s])); G = 100 - p["#4"]; F = p["#200"]; S = 100 - F - G
    frac[s] = (G, S, F)

def logterp(x, di, Pi, dj, Pj):
    """Deck formula: log(Dx/di) = log(di/dj)/(Pi-Pj) * (x-Pi).  i = coarser sieve."""
    return di * 10 ** (math.log10(di / dj) / (Pi - Pj) * (x - Pi))

pts = [(op[s], PP[2][i]) for i, s in enumerate(sv)]
D = {}
for x in (60, 30, 10):
    for (di, Pi), (dj, Pj) in zip(pts, pts[1:]):
        if Pj <= x <= Pi:
            D[x] = logterp(x, di, Pi, dj, Pj); break
Cu, Cc = D[60] / D[10], D[30] ** 2 / (D[10] * D[60])

# the two error modes step 9 is scaffolded against
bad_Cu = D[10] / D[60]                      # inverted: always < 1, self-catching
bad_Cc = D[30] ** 2 / (D[10] + D[60])       # sum instead of product: still < 1, silent

def fine_name(sym, G, S):
    """ASTM D2487 Table 4 group-name logic for fine-grained soils."""
    b = {"CL-ML": ("silty clay", "Silty clay"), "CH": ("fat clay", "Fat clay")}[sym]
    c = G + S
    if c < 15: return b[1]
    if c < 30: return f"{b[1]} with {'sand' if S >= G else 'gravel'}"
    if S >= G: return f"Sandy {b[0]}" + ("" if G < 15 else " with gravel")
    return f"Gravelly {b[0]}" + ("" if S < 15 else " with sand")
```

Every value was recomputed from the stated percent-finer table; nothing is transcribed, and all three group symbols and all three group names were produced by the ASTM D2487 threshold logic coded above rather than by pattern-matching the deck's existing answers — they then agreed with them exactly, which is the cross-check. Three independent consistency tests were applied. The plasticity data is internally consistent: $PI = LL - PL$ holds for both soils that have limits, $20 - 15 = 5$ and $124 - 47 = 77$, so neither row contains a typo. Each soil's three fractions sum to $100\ \%$. And Soil 3's limits sit below the U-line ($77 < 104$), which is the standard test that a liquid limit as extreme as $124\ \%$ is a real measurement rather than a botched one — worth running precisely because that value looks wrong at first sight.

Soil 2's three sizes were obtained by logarithmic interpolation, an exact route that never touches a figure, and each was checked to fall strictly between its bracketing sieve openings — a test that catches an inverted interpolation when the arithmetic alone would not. The interpolation formula was verified at both endpoints: substituting $x = P_{P,i}$ returns $d_i$ and $x = P_{P,i+1}$ returns $d_{i+1}$ to six decimal places. Note that $D_{30}$ and $D_{10}$ both fall in the same bracket, between the No. 100 and No. 40 sieves, where $32\ \%$ of the soil sits in a single half-decade of size — that segment carries two of the three sizes, so an error reading it propagates into both coefficients.

The plasticity chart's drawn extent was decoded from the figure's own axis ticks rather than assumed: the SVG is matplotlib output with its text converted to glyph references, but each tick label survives as an XML comment, giving $LL \in [0, 111]$ and $PI \in [0, 61]$. Soil 3 at $LL = 124$, $PI = 77$ is therefore off the chart on **both** axes, which is why step 14 uses the A-line equation. That is a verified fact about this deck's figure, not a general statement about plasticity charts.

Units: all sizes in millimetres; $C_u$ and $C_c$ dimensionless as ratios of lengths and of length² over length²; every fraction and limit a percentage. No conversion appears anywhere in the example. Magnitude checks: Soil 1 at $60\ \%$ fines with $PI = 5$ is a lean, barely-plastic silty clay with a substantial sand tail, matching "sandy silty clay"; Soil 2 with $92\ \%$ sand, $C_u = 4.93$ spanning only $0.160$ to $0.790\ \mathrm{mm}$, is a narrowly-graded fine-to-medium sand, which is what "poorly graded" asserts; Soil 3 at $97\ \%$ fines with $LL = 124\ \%$ is an extremely plastic clay, consistent with "fat clay" and with a montmorillonitic mineralogy — the connection the following deck picks up in its activity examples.

## Scaffolding decisions

**Three parts, one per soil, matching the deck's existing three `ProblemBox` slides.** `data-part="1"` on steps 1–4, `"2"` on steps 5–11, `"3"` on steps 12–15. Step numbering runs 1–15 continuously because the plugin carries the counter across parts, and continuity is the point: the same four questions are asked three times, and seeing step 5 repeat step 1 on a different soil is what makes the procedure look like a procedure. Fifteen steps is far past the usual six to eight, but this is three complete classifications rather than one problem, and no slide carries more than seven.

**Step 1 is deliberately the same shape on all three soils** — three fractions, all blue, obtained by the same two reads and one subtraction. It is the only step that repeats verbatim, and repeating it is cheaper than a cross-reference. The $100 - P_P$ versus $P_P$ contrast introduced back in Example 2.5 is exercised three more times here without a fresh note.

**Steps 2, 6 and 13 obtain no number and still earn their place.** Each is the single branch that decides which half of the classification system applies, and a student who takes it wrongly produces a plausible-looking answer with no internal contradiction to catch it. Making the branch a numbered step, with the test written out as $F_F \geq 50\ \%$ in black and the verdict in blue, forces the comparison to be made explicitly rather than assumed from the soil's appearance.

**Step 3 blanks the A-line substitution, not just the symbol.** $LL = 20\ \%$ makes $PI_A = 0$ exactly, so the comparison $PI \geq PI_A$ is trivially satisfied and a student can reach CL-ML by looking at the chart's hatched corner without ever computing the A-line. Blanking $0.73(20 - 20)$ makes them evaluate it, which is what step 14 then requires them to do for real. The dual symbol CL-ML is green.

**Step 4, 11 and 15 are the group-name steps, and they are where the marks actually go.** Each pre-prints its threshold tests in black — $G_F + S_F$ against $30\ \%$, $S_F$ against $G_F$, $G_F$ against $15\ \%$ — with the verdicts in blue and the name in green. The three soils were not chosen to be alike here and the solution should not flatten them: Soil 1 passes the $30\ \%$ gate and needs a secondary descriptor, Soil 3 fails the $15\ \%$ gate and takes no descriptor at all, and Soil 2 runs the coarse-grained branch of the table instead. Three worked outcomes from one rule set is the most transferable thing in the example.

**Step 8 has no blank for the plot itself.** Soil 2's curve is drawn live on `Modeule2_GSD_plot.svg` on the slide before, so the step records the three readings only, in a small `.sol-table` with the percentage pre-printed in black and $D_x$ blank, so the student enters the graph three times rather than copying a list. All three cells share one `data-step` so the row reveals on a single click.

**Step 9 blanks both substitutions rather than the results**, for the reason established in Example 2.6: an inverted $C_u$ gives $D_{10}/D_{60} = 0.203$, which is visibly impossible because $C_u \geq 1$ by construction, and the student catches it unaided. But $C_c$ written with a sum in the denominator gives $0.0992$ instead of $0.745$ — still less than 1, still "poorly graded", still SP, and therefore completely invisible in the answer. Only a blank on the substitution distinguishes the two. Both coefficients are blue and the letter P is green.

**Step 10 is short and stays separate from step 9** because the two letters of SP-SM come from different data. P comes from the sieve curve, M comes from the plasticity column, and a student who has just spent two steps on gradation will reach for the gradation again if the fines letter is folded in beside it.

**Step 14 carries the U-line check as black scaffolding, not as a blank.** It is a validity test rather than a decision, but it is the reason to trust $LL = 124\ \%$, and printing it teaches that the chart has an upper bound that real soils do not cross.

Red notes, at most one per step. (2) $F_F \geq 50\ \%$ is the whole fork — above it the sieve curve is irrelevant, below it the limits are; (3) the A-line starts at $LL = 20$, so $PI_A = 0$ here and *any* plastic soil plots above it; (4) the "sandy"/"gravelly" prefix needs $\geq 30\ \%$ coarse but "with sand"/"with gravel" needs only $\geq 15\ \%$ — two different gates, checked in that order; (7) between $5\ \%$ and $12\ \%$ fines you need *both* letters, and $F_F = 5\ \%$ is inside the band, not below it; (9) $C_c$ is $D_{30}$ squared over the **product** of the other two, and a well-graded sand must pass both tests, not either; (10) non-plastic fines are silt, so the second letter is M — "no plasticity" does not mean "no fines letter"; (14) this soil is off the printed chart on both axes, so the A-line equation is the only route.

Two notes exceed the roughly 110-character budget and take `data-slide` short forms. Step 4: "Two gates — 30 % coarse for *sandy*, 15 % gravel for *with gravel*. Check in that order." Step 14: "$LL=124$, $PI=77$ are off this chart ($LL\leq110$, $PI\leq60$) — use $PI_A=0.73(LL-20)$."

**No answer is restated in a note.** Step 9's note names the error mode without giving $0.745$, and step 14's note gives the chart bounds without giving $75.9$ — the two places the leak check would otherwise fire.
