## Statement

> For the element in the figure below find: (a) the principal stresses and their orientation using the analytical solutions, (b) the stresses acting on a plane oriented 25 deg. from the horizontal. (c) Check the results using the Mohr's circle method.

The element is `FiguresGeneral/example62.svg`: $300\ \mathrm{kPa}$ on the horizontal faces, $200\ \mathrm{kPa}$ on the vertical faces, $75\ \mathrm{kPa}$ shear on all four faces, and a plane at $25^\circ$ from the horizontal.

## Given / Find

- $\sigma_y = 300\ \mathrm{kPa}$, $\sigma_x = 200\ \mathrm{kPa}$, $\tau_{xy} = -75\ \mathrm{kPa}$ (clockwise), $\alpha = 25^\circ$
- **Find:** (a) $\sigma_1$, $\sigma_3$ and their orientation; (b) $\sigma_\alpha$ and $\tau_\alpha$ on the $25^\circ$ plane; (c) the same results from Mohr's circle

## Assumptions

The sign convention is the one on the handwritten slides: $\tau_{xy} = -75\ \mathrm{kPa}$ because the shear is clockwise, $\alpha$ is measured counterclockwise from the horizontal, and the stresses on a plane follow $\sigma_\alpha = \dfrac{\sigma_x+\sigma_y}{2} + \dfrac{\sigma_y-\sigma_x}{2}\cos 2\alpha + \tau_{xy}\sin 2\alpha$ and $\tau_\alpha = \dfrac{\sigma_y-\sigma_x}{2}\sin 2\alpha - \tau_{xy}\cos 2\alpha$. These are the author's annotations, already on the slide.

## Solution

The slides solve the problem two ways. Method 1 uses the full equations for the stresses on a plane. Method 2 uses the mean and deviatoric stresses with the angle measured from the principal plane. Part (c) plots Mohr's circle to check both.

**Slide 1 — data and principles** (beside the element figure)

1. Data — $\sigma_y = 300\ \mathrm{kPa}$, $\sigma_x = 200\ \mathrm{kPa}$, $\tau_{xy} = -75\ \mathrm{kPa}$ (author's red note: clockwise)
2. Principles — many ways to solve. 1) Using the full analytical solutions: $\sigma_\alpha = \dfrac{\sigma_x+\sigma_y}{2} + \dfrac{\sigma_y-\sigma_x}{2}\cos(2\alpha) + \tau_{xy}\sin(2\alpha)$ and $\tau_\alpha = \dfrac{\sigma_y-\sigma_x}{2}\sin(2\alpha) - \tau_{xy}\cos(2\alpha)$

**Slide 2 — (a) principal stresses, method 1**

3. Orientation — $\tau_\alpha = 0$ (author's red note: what $\alpha$ makes $\tau = 0$?), so $\tan(2\alpha) = \dfrac{2\tau_{xy}}{\sigma_y-\sigma_x}$ and $\alpha = \dfrac{1}{2}\arctan\left(\dfrac{2\tau_{xy}}{\sigma_y-\sigma_x}\right) = 0.5\arctan\left(\dfrac{2(-75)}{300-200}\right) = \mathbf{-28.2^\circ}$ (author's red note: be careful to preserve sign convention)
4. Major principal stress — $\sigma_\alpha = \dfrac{200+300}{2} + \dfrac{300-200}{2}\cos(-2 \times 28.2) + (-75)\sin(-2 \times 28.2) = 250 + 27.7 + 62.4 = \mathbf{340.1\ \mathrm{kPa}}$
5. Second principal direction — rotated-system sketch kept as a figure; $\beta = 90^\circ + \alpha$ (author's red note: orthogonal direction), so $\beta = 90 - 28.2 = 61.8^\circ$
6. Minor principal stress — $\sigma_\beta = \dfrac{200+300}{2} + \dfrac{300-200}{2}\cos(2 \times 61.8) + (-75)\sin(2 \times 61.8) = 250 - 27.7 - 62.4 = \mathbf{160\ \mathrm{kPa}}$
7. Therefore — $\sigma_1 = \mathbf{340.1\ \mathrm{kPa}}$ and $\sigma_3 = \mathbf{160\ \mathrm{kPa}}$

**Slide 3 — (b) by method 1, then method 2**

8. (b) Normal stress on the $25^\circ$ plane — $\sigma_\alpha = \dfrac{200+300}{2} + \dfrac{300-200}{2}\cos(2 \times 25) + (-75)\sin(2 \times 25) = 250 + 32.1 - 57.5 = \mathbf{225\ \mathrm{kPa}}$
9. (b) Shear stress on the $25^\circ$ plane — $\tau_\alpha = \dfrac{300-200}{2}\sin(2 \times 25) - (-75)\cos(2 \times 25) = 38.3 + 48.2 = \mathbf{86.5\ \mathrm{kPa}}$
10. 2) Use simplified equations. First find mean and deviatoric stresses — $\sigma_m = 0.5(\sigma_x+\sigma_y) = 0.5(200+300) = 250\ \mathrm{kPa}$ and $\sigma_d = \sqrt{(\sigma_x-\sigma_y)^2 + 4\tau_{xy}^2} = \sqrt{(200-300)^2 + 4 \times 75^2} = 180.3\ \mathrm{kPa}$
11. (a) Principal stresses — $\sigma_{1,3} = \sigma_m \pm 0.5\sigma_d$, so $\sigma_1 = 250 + 0.5 \times 180.3 = \mathbf{340.1\ \mathrm{kPa}}$ and $\sigma_3 = 250 - 0.5 \times 180.3 = \mathbf{160\ \mathrm{kPa}}$

**Slide 4 — (b) by method 2, and (c) Mohr's circle**

12. (b) Normal stress — $\sigma_\alpha = \sigma_m + 0.5\sigma_d\cos(2\alpha)$ (author's red note: in this equation the angle is measured from the principal stresses; the rotated-system sketch is repeated here) $= 250 + 0.5 \times 180.3\cos(2(25+28.2))$ (author's red note under $25+28.2$: angle to rotate from the principal stress configuration) $= \mathbf{225\ \mathrm{kPa}}$
13. (b) Shear stress — $\tau_\alpha = 0.5\sigma_d\sin(2\alpha) = 0.5 \times 180.3\sin(2(25+28.2)) = \mathbf{86.5\ \mathrm{kPa}}$
14. (c) Plot Mohr's circle — kept as the handwritten graphical solution: points $(200, -75)$ and $(300, 75)$, the diameter between them, $-56^\circ$ from it to $\sigma_1$, $\sigma_3 = 160\ \mathrm{kPa}$ and $\sigma_1 = 340\ \mathrm{kPa}$ on the axis, $50^\circ$ to the point $(225, 87)$

Units are kPa throughout and the trigonometric terms are dimensionless. $\tau_\alpha = 0$ at $\alpha = -28.2^\circ$ and at $\beta = 61.8^\circ$, which confirms the principal directions.
