## Statement

> A modified Proctor test results in the data shown in the table below. Determine: (i) the maximum dry density, (ii) the optimum water content, (iii) the void ratio and saturation at the maximum dry density, and (iv) plot the data with the ZAV and a line passing through the saturation at the optimum water content. Assume $G_s=2.67$

| $\gamma_d$ (pcf) | $w$ (%) |
|---|---|
| 99.5 | 10 |
| 104 | 14 |
| 105.5 | 16 |
| 106 | 18 |
| 105 | 20 |
| 103.5 | 22 |
| 101 | 24 |

## Given / Find

- The table above, $G_s = 2.67$
- **Find:** $\gamma_{d,max}$, $w_{opt}$, $e$ and $S$ at the peak, and the plot

## Assumptions

The peak is read from a smooth curve through the data, as in the handwritten solution. The curve is a natural cubic spline through the test points. It peaks near $w_{opt} = 17.6\%$, not at the $18\%$ of the highest tested point.

$\gamma_{d,max}$ is rounded to $106\ \mathrm{pcf}$ for the calculations, as in the handwritten solution. The plot prints the unrounded $106.03\ \mathrm{pcf}$.

$\gamma_w = 62.4\ \mathrm{pcf}$.

## Solution

The peak of the curve gives $\gamma_{d,max}$ and $w_{opt}$. The void ratio and saturation then come from the phase relationships $\gamma_d = \dfrac{\gamma_w G_s}{1 + e}$ and $Se = wG_s$. The theoretical lines on the plot come from $\gamma_{d,theory} = \dfrac{\gamma_w G_s}{1 + wG_s/S}$.

1. Plot, part (iv) — the data with a smooth curve, the ZAV ($S = 100\%$), and the line through the peak ($S = 82.3\%$)
2. Parts (i) and (ii), according to the plot — $\gamma_{d,max} = \mathbf{106\ \mathrm{pcf}}$ and $w_{opt} = \mathbf{17.6\%}$
3. Void ratio, part (iii) — $\gamma_d = \dfrac{\gamma_w G_s}{1 + e} \Rightarrow 1 + e = \dfrac{\gamma_w}{\gamma_d}G_s = \dfrac{62.4}{106} \times 2.67 = 1.572$, so $e = \mathbf{0.572}$
4. Saturation, part (iii) — $S = \dfrac{wG_s}{e} = \dfrac{0.176 \times 2.67}{0.572} = 0.822$, so $S = \mathbf{82.2\%}$
