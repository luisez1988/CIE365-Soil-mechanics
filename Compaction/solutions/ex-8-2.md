## Statement

> Find the compaction effort of a Proctor test knowing $h_d=45.7$cm, $m_h=4.54$kg, $N_b=25$, $N_l=5$. Compute the result in lb-ft/ft$^3$ and kJ/m$^3$. The volume of the compacted soil is 943 cm$^3$

## Given / Find

- $h_d = 45.7\ \mathrm{cm}$, $m_h = 4.54\ \mathrm{kg}$, $N_b = 25$ per layer, $N_l = 5$, $V = 943\ \mathrm{cm^3}$
- **Find:** $E_{comp}$ in $\mathrm{kJ/m^3}$ and in $\mathrm{lb\text{-}ft/ft^3}$

## Assumptions

None — the statement is complete. $g = 9.81\ \mathrm{m/s^2}$.

For the US units, the test values are taken as the nominal hammer and mold of the standard: $h_d = 18\ \mathrm{in}$, $m_h = 10\ \mathrm{lb}$, $V = 1/30\ \mathrm{ft^3}$. This follows the handwritten solution.

## Solution

The compaction effort is the energy delivered per unit volume, $E_{comp} = m_h g\,\dfrac{h_d N_b N_l}{V}$. The equation is unit-consistent, so the units of the result come from the units put in.

1. Substitute in SI units — $E_{comp} = (4.54 \times 9.81) \times \dfrac{45.7 \times 25 \times 5}{943} = 270\ \mathrm{N\,cm/cm^3}$
2. Convert to $\mathrm{kJ/m^3}$ — $E_{comp} = 270\ \mathrm{N\,cm/cm^3} \times \dfrac{(100\ \mathrm{cm})^2}{\mathrm{m^2}} = 2{,}700{,}000\ \mathrm{N\,m/m^3} = \mathbf{2{,}700\ \mathrm{kJ/m^3}}$
3. Convert the data to US units — $h_d = 45.7\ \mathrm{cm} = 18\ \mathrm{in}$, $m_h = 4.54\ \mathrm{kg} = 10\ \mathrm{lb}$, $V = \dfrac{1}{30}\ \mathrm{ft^3}$
4. Substitute in US units — $E_{comp} = 10 \times \dfrac{(18/12) \times 25 \times 5}{1/30} = \mathbf{56{,}250\ \mathrm{lb\text{-}ft/ft^3}}$

## Verification

```
mh, hd, Nb, Nl, V = 4.54, 45.7, 25, 5, 943
E = mh*9.81*hd*Nb*Nl/V            # N cm/cm3
E_US = 10*(18/12)*25*5/(1/30)     # lb-ft/ft3

--- Ex 8.2
mh g=44.54 N  hd Nb Nl/V=6.058 cm/cm3  E=269.80 N cm/cm3
E=2697984 N m/m3 = 2698 kJ/m3 ; with Nl=3: 161.9 N cm/cm3
hd=17.99 in  mh=10.01 lb  V=0.03330 ft3 (1/30=0.03333)
E_US=56250 lb-ft/ft3 ; Nl=3: 33750
```

Each value was recomputed in Python. Units: $\mathrm{N \cdot cm/cm^3}$ times $10^4\ \mathrm{cm^2/m^2}$ gives $\mathrm{N \cdot m/m^3}$, and $1\ \mathrm{N \cdot m} = 1\ \mathrm{J}$. Converting $2{,}698\ \mathrm{kJ/m^3}$ directly gives about $56{,}350\ \mathrm{lb\text{-}ft/ft^3}$, which agrees with step 4 to within the rounding of the nominal values. The result matches the modified Proctor effort, about $2{,}700\ \mathrm{kJ/m^3}$.
