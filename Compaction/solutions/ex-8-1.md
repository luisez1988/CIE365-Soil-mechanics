## Statement

> Calculate the relative density of uniform spherical particles when their void ratio is 0.5. What is the maximum and minimum dry densities of such soil? Assume $G_s=2.65$

The porosities come from the table in `FiguresGeneral/spheres.svg`, on the slide before "Relative density": $47.6\%$ for the cubic packing and $26.0\%$ for the rhombohedral packing.

## Given / Find

- $e = 0.5$, $G_s = 2.65$
- From `spheres.svg`: $n_{max} = 0.476$, $n_{min} = 0.26$
- **Find:** $D_r$, $\gamma_{d,max}$, $\gamma_{d,min}$

## Assumptions

The loosest and densest states of uniform spheres are the cubic and rhombohedral packings. The statement does not give the limits, and the handwritten solution takes them from these two packings.

The "dry densities" are reported as dry unit weights in pcf with $\gamma_w = 62.4\ \mathrm{pcf}$. This follows the handwritten solution.

## Solution

The relative density compares the current void ratio with the two limiting void ratios, $D_r = \dfrac{e_{max} - e}{e_{max} - e_{min}} \times 100$. The dry unit weight follows from the phase relationship $\gamma_d = \dfrac{\gamma_w G_s}{1 + e}$.

1. Limiting void ratios — recall $n = \dfrac{e}{1 + e}$, so $e = \dfrac{n}{1 - n}$. Then $e_{max} = \dfrac{0.476}{0.524} = 0.908$ and $e_{min} = \dfrac{0.26}{0.74} = 0.351$
2. Relative density — $D_r = \dfrac{0.908 - 0.5}{0.908 - 0.351} \times 100 = \mathbf{73.3\%}$
3. Maximum dry unit weight — $\gamma_{d,max} = \dfrac{\gamma_w G_s}{1 + e_{min}} = \dfrac{62.4 \times 2.65}{1 + 0.351} = \mathbf{122\ \mathrm{pcf}}$
4. Minimum dry unit weight — $\gamma_{d,min} = \dfrac{\gamma_w G_s}{1 + e_{max}} = \dfrac{62.4 \times 2.65}{1 + 0.908} = \mathbf{86.7\ \mathrm{pcf}}$

## Verification

```
nmax, nmin, e, Gs = 0.476, 0.26, 0.5, 2.65
emax, emin = nmax/(1-nmax), nmin/(1-nmin)
Dr = (emax-e)/(emax-emin)*100
gd_max, gd_min = 62.4*Gs/(1+emin), 62.4*Gs/(1+emax)

--- Ex 8.1
emax=0.9084  emin=0.3514
Dr=73.31 %   rounded: 73.25 %
gd_max=122.37 pcf  gd_min=86.65 pcf
rounded: 122.40 pcf, 86.67 pcf
SI: 19.24 kN/m3, 13.62 kN/m3
```

Each value was recomputed in Python from the unrounded porosities and from the rounded void ratios on the slide. Units: $e$ and $n$ are dimensionless, so $\gamma_d$ carries the units of $\gamma_w$. Both limits are typical of sands, and $e = 0.5$ falls between them, so $D_r$ lies between $0$ and $100\%$ as it must.
