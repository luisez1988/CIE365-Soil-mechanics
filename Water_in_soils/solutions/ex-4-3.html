Holding \(\sigma_{v,A}\) fixed while the water table moves assumes \(\gamma_t\approx\gamma_{sat}\) for the soil right above the table, reasonable because capillary rise keeps it nearly saturated even without submergence. That is not automatic: capillary rise only reaches so far above the table, a coarse sand may stay wet for a few inches while a fine silt or clay can stay wet for several feet. If the drop in the water table is large next to that height, the soil really does dry out, \(\gamma_t\) falls below \(\gamma_{sat}\), and this shortcut should be replaced with a real drained unit weight.
New water table depth — lowered 3 ft \(h_{w,1}=z_A-(z_{GWT}+3)=\) \(23.5-(10+3)\) \(=\) 10.5 \(\unit{ft}\)
Pore pressure at A \(u_{A,1}=h_{w,1}\gw=\) \(10.5\times62.4\) \(=\) 655.2 \(\unit{psf}\)
Effective vertical stress at A \(\sigma'_{v,A,1}=\sigma_{v,A}-u_{A,1}=\) \(2822.5-655.2\) \(=\) 2167.3 \(\unit{psf}\)
Effect \(\Delta\sigma'_v=2167.3-1980.1=+187.2\unit{psf}\), an increase: equivalent to loading the soil at A, so it will settle. A falling water table always raises \(\sigma'_v\) — the sign is known before computing.
New water table depth — at the surface \(h_{w,2}=z_A-0=\) 23.5 \(\unit{ft}\)
Pore pressure at A \(u_{A,2}=h_{w,2}\gw=\) \(23.5\times62.4\) \(=\) 1466.4 \(\unit{psf}\)
Effective vertical stress at A \(\sigma'_{v,A,2}=\sigma_{v,A}-u_{A,2}=\) \(2822.5-1466.4\) \(=\) 1356.1 \(\unit{psf}\)
Effect \(\Delta\sigma'_v=1356.1-1980.1=-624.0\unit{psf}\), a decrease: equivalent to unloading the soil at A, so it will heave. A rising water table always lowers \(\sigma'_v\) — the opposite sign from case 1.
