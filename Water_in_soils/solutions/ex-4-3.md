## Statement

> What is the effect of lowering the GWT 3 ft on the effective vertical stresses for the soil profile in Example 4.1. What if the GWT is at the surface?

## Given / Find

- Soil profile and point A as in Example 4.1: CL ($0$–$10\ \mathrm{ft}$, $\gamma_t = 115\ \mathrm{pcf}$), MH ($10$–$20\ \mathrm{ft}$, $\gamma_{sat} = 120\ \mathrm{pcf}$), SM ($20$–$30\ \mathrm{ft}$, $\gamma_{sat} = 135\ \mathrm{pcf}$), point A at $z_A = 23.5\ \mathrm{ft}$
- Baseline (Example 4.1), original water table at $z = 10\ \mathrm{ft}$: $\sigma_{v,A} = 2822.5\ \mathrm{psf}$, $u_{A,0} = 842.4\ \mathrm{psf}$, $\sigma'_{v,A,0} = 1980.1\ \mathrm{psf}$
- Case 1: water table lowered by $3\ \mathrm{ft}$, new depth $= 13\ \mathrm{ft}$
- Case 2: water table at the ground surface, depth $= 0\ \mathrm{ft}$
- **Find:** $\sigma'_{v,A}$ in each case, and whether the change corresponds to loading or unloading of the soil at A

## Assumptions

Lowering the water table dries out the soil that used to sit below it, which in principle changes that soil's unit weight from $\gamma_{sat}$ toward $\gamma_t$ and would lower $\sigma_{v,A}$ a little. Right above a water table, capillary rise keeps the pores close to full even without submergence, so it is reasonable to take $\gamma_t \approx \gamma_{sat}$ for that zone and hold the total vertical stress at A fixed at the Example 4.1 value, $\sigma_{v,A} = 2822.5\ \mathrm{psf}$, in both cases, only $u_A$ is recalculated from the new water table position.

This assumption should be checked, not applied automatically. Capillary rise only reaches so far above the water table, set mostly by grain size and pore size, a coarse sand may stay wet for just a few inches while a fine silt or clay can stay wet for several feet. If the drop in the water table is large next to that capillary rise height, the soil well above the new table really does dry out, $\gamma_t$ falls noticeably below $\gamma_{sat}$, and holding $\sigma_{v,A}$ fixed stops being a good approximation. Before reusing this shortcut on a different profile, compare the size of the drop to the soil's capillary rise and use a real drained unit weight if the two are not close. Raising the water table to the surface, case 2, does not have this problem, soil that becomes submerged only gets wetter, so $\sigma_{v,A}$ staying fixed is the safer direction of the same assumption.

## Solution

In both cases $u_A$ is recomputed from the new depth of the water table using $u = h_w \gamma_w$, and $\sigma'_{v,A}$ follows from $\sigma'_v = \sigma_v - u$ with $\sigma_v$ held at the Example 4.1 value; comparing the new $\sigma'_{v,A}$ to the baseline $1980.1\ \mathrm{psf}$ tells whether the soil at A is being effectively loaded or unloaded.

1. New water table depth, case 1 (lowered $3\ \mathrm{ft}$) — $z_{GWT,1} = 10 + 3 = 13\ \mathrm{ft}$, so $h_{w,1} = 23.5 - 13 = 10.5\ \mathrm{ft}$
2. Pore pressure at A, case 1 — $u_{A,1} = h_{w,1}\gamma_w = 10.5 \times 62.4 = 655.2\ \mathrm{psf}$
3. Effective vertical stress at A, case 1 — $\sigma'_{v,A,1} = \sigma_{v,A} - u_{A,1} = 2822.5 - 655.2 = \mathbf{2167.3\ \mathrm{psf}}$
4. Effect, case 1 — $\Delta\sigma'_v = 2167.3 - 1980.1 = +187.2\ \mathrm{psf}$, an **increase**: equivalent to loading the soil at A, so it will cause settlement
5. New water table depth, case 2 (at the surface) — $h_{w,2} = 23.5 - 0 = 23.5\ \mathrm{ft}$
6. Pore pressure at A, case 2 — $u_{A,2} = h_{w,2}\gamma_w = 23.5 \times 62.4 = 1466.4\ \mathrm{psf}$
7. Effective vertical stress at A, case 2 — $\sigma'_{v,A,2} = \sigma_{v,A} - u_{A,2} = 2822.5 - 1466.4 = \mathbf{1356.1\ \mathrm{psf}}$
8. Effect, case 2 — $\Delta\sigma'_v = 1356.1 - 1980.1 = -624.0\ \mathrm{psf}$, a **decrease**: equivalent to unloading the soil at A, so it will cause heave

## Verification

```
baseline: sigma_v,A = 2822.5 psf, u_A0 = 842.4 psf, sigma'_v,A0 = 1980.1 psf

case 1 (GWT lowered 3 ft, new depth 13 ft):
h_w1 = 23.5 - 13                = 10.5 ft
u_A1 = 10.5 * 62.4              = 655.2 psf
sigma'_v,A1 = 2822.5 - 655.2    = 2167.3 psf
delta = 2167.3 - 1980.1         = +187.2 psf  -> loading, settlement

case 2 (GWT at surface, depth 0 ft):
h_w2 = 23.5 - 0                 = 23.5 ft
u_A2 = 23.5 * 62.4              = 1466.4 psf
sigma'_v,A2 = 2822.5 - 1466.4   = 1356.1 psf
delta = 1356.1 - 1980.1         = -624.0 psf  -> unloading, heave
```

Every value recomputed independently in Python. The legacy `EX460.svg` reports $\sigma'_{v,A,1} = 2168\ \mathrm{psf}$ and $\sigma'_{v,A,2} = 1357\ \mathrm{psf}$, one unit above each value here for the same reason as Examples 4.1 and 4.2: it carries the rounded $\sigma_{v,A} = 2823\ \mathrm{psf}$ forward instead of the unrounded $2822.5\ \mathrm{psf}$. The direction of both effects checks out physically: lowering the water table removes buoyant support and raises effective stress (soils consolidate when the water table drops, a mechanism behind land subsidence from groundwater pumping); raising it to the surface adds buoyant support everywhere and lowers effective stress, which is why a rising water table can trigger heave.

Steps 2, 3, 6, 7 follow the same black-equation, blue-substitution pattern as Example 4.1, reusing that example's structure since the underlying calculation is identical, only the water table depth differs.

Green marks the two case answers, $\sigma'_{v,A,1}$ and $\sigma'_{v,A,2}$, and the two interpretive conclusions (loading/settlement, unloading/heave) in steps 4 and 8, since the interpretation is as much the point of this example as the numbers are.

Red notes: one on the assumption itself, holding $\sigma_{v,A}$ fixed relies on capillary rise keeping $\gamma_t \approx \gamma_{sat}$ near the water table, and that should be checked against the size of the drop before it is reused on another profile; one on step 4 or 8 pointing out the general rule, a falling water table always increases effective stress and a rising one always decreases it, regardless of the numbers, so the sign can be predicted before computing.
