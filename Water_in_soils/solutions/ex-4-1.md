## Statement

> Compute the total and effective vertical stresses at point A. Plot the vertical total and effective stresses with the pore pressure profile in the same graph.

## Given / Find

- Layer 1, CL: $0$–$10\ \mathrm{ft}$ below the ground surface, $\gamma_t = 115\ \mathrm{pcf}$, above the water table
- Layer 2, MH: $10$–$20\ \mathrm{ft}$, $\gamma_{sat} = 120\ \mathrm{pcf}$, below the water table
- Layer 3, SM: $20$–$30\ \mathrm{ft}$, $\gamma_{sat} = 135\ \mathrm{pcf}$, below the water table
- Ground water table at $z = 10\ \mathrm{ft}$, coinciding with the CL/MH interface
- Point A at $z = 23.5\ \mathrm{ft}$, $3.5\ \mathrm{ft}$ into the SM layer
- **Find:** $\sigma_{v,A}$, $\sigma'_{v,A}$, and the total stress, pore pressure, and effective stress profile with depth

## Assumptions

The problem statement's text box carries no numbers at all, the layer thicknesses, unit weights, water table position, and the depth of point A are all read off the figure `dexample_stress.svg`, since the legacy `EX440.svg` is a handwritten answer key rather than machine-readable geometry. The unit weight of water is taken as $\gamma_w = 62.4\ \mathrm{pcf}$, the standard value, since it is not stated on the slide. The CL layer sits above the water table, so its pore pressure is zero and its own weight is carried using the given total unit weight $\gamma_t$ directly, no assumption about saturation is needed there.

## Solution

The total vertical stress at a depth is the weight of everything above it, so the profile is built layer by layer with $\sigma_v = \sum_i \gamma_i h_i$; the pore pressure follows the hydrostatic rule $u = h_w \gamma_w$ measured from the water table, and the effective stress is recovered from the principle of effective stress, $\sigma' = \sigma - u$.

1. Depth of point A below the water table — $h_w = z_A - z_{GWT} = 23.5 - 10 = 13.5\ \mathrm{ft}$
2. Total vertical stress at A — $\sigma_{v,A} = \gamma_{CL} h_{CL} + \gamma_{MH} h_{MH} + \gamma_{SM} h_{SM,A} = 115(10) + 120(10) + 135(3.5) = 2822.5\ \mathrm{psf}$
3. Pore pressure at A — $u_A = h_w \gamma_w = 13.5 \times 62.4 = 842.4\ \mathrm{psf}$
4. Effective vertical stress at A — $\sigma'_{v,A} = \sigma_{v,A} - u_A = 2822.5 - 842.4 = \mathbf{1980.1\ \mathrm{psf}}$
5. Stress profile for the plot, evaluated at the layer interfaces and at A:

| $z$ (ft) | $\sigma_v$ (psf) | $u$ (psf) | $\sigma'_v$ (psf) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 0.0 |
| 10 | 1150.0 | 0.0 | 1150.0 |
| 20 | 2350.0 | 624.0 | 1726.0 |
| 23.5 (A) | 2822.5 | 842.4 | 1980.1 |
| 30 | 3700.0 | 1248.0 | 2452.0 |

## Verification

```
gamma_w = 62.4 pcf
sigma_v,A = 115*10 + 120*10 + 135*3.5      = 2822.5 psf
h_w       = 23.5 - 10                       = 13.5 ft
u_A       = 13.5 * 62.4                     = 842.4 psf
sigma'_v,A= 2822.5 - 842.4                  = 1980.1 psf

profile:
z= 0.0  sigma_v=   0.00  u=   0.00  sigma'_v=   0.00
z=10.0  sigma_v=1150.00  u=   0.00  sigma'_v=1150.00
z=20.0  sigma_v=2350.00  u= 624.00  sigma'_v=1726.00
z=23.5  sigma_v=2822.50  u= 842.40  sigma'_v=1980.10
z=30.0  sigma_v=3700.00  u=1248.00  sigma'_v=2452.00
```

Every value recomputed independently in Python, matching the hand values above. The legacy `EX440.svg` reports $\sigma_{v,A} = 2823\ \mathrm{psf}$ and $\sigma'_{v,A} = 1981\ \mathrm{psf}$, one pound-per-square-foot off because it rounds $\sigma_{v,A}$ to the nearest integer before subtracting $u_A$; the unrounded values above are used on the slide. Units check dimensionally on every line: $\mathrm{pcf} \times \mathrm{ft} = \mathrm{psf}$ throughout. Magnitude check: about $2800\ \mathrm{psf}$ ($\approx 1.35\ \mathrm{tsf}$) under $23.5\ \mathrm{ft}$ of soil with unit weights of $115$–$135\ \mathrm{pcf}$ is the right order of magnitude.

## Scaffolding decisions

Step 1 is bookkeeping (a subtraction of two given depths) and is a light blue fill, not a full black step, since no decision is required beyond knowing depth is measured from the water table.

Steps 2–4 are black for the governing sum/subtraction with blue fills for each substituted term, so the student decides which unit weight and which thickness belongs to which layer.

Green marks $\sigma_{v,A}$ and $\sigma'_{v,A}$, the two quantities the problem asks for by name.

The profile table (step 5) is scaffolded as a fill-in table: $z$ and the interface identity are pre-printed, the three stress columns are blanks, since reading a stress profile off a plot is the actual skill being built here, and it also reuses the point-A numbers the student just computed.

Red notes: one on step 2 flagging that the CL layer uses $\gamma_t$, not $\gamma_{sat}$, because it sits above the water table (the classic mistake is saturating the whole column); one on step 3 flagging that $h_w$ is measured from the water table, not from the ground surface.
