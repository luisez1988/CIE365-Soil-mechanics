## Statement

> A landfill is designed to contain waste ($\gamma_t=112$ pcf) up to a height of 30 ft over a thick clay ($\gamma_{sat}=131$ pcf) as shown in the figure. The water table is at the top of the clay. The landfill is wide in comparison to the thickness of the soil layer. Calculate the increment of stress, and the final (long-term) total and effective vertical stresses at a depth of 5 ft.

Revised on the author's request. The original read "The landfill wide in comparison" and gave the unit weights and water table only in the figure.

The statement is figure-dependent. Values read from the figure (`FiguresGeneral/landfill.svg`, and the annotated PDF supplied by the author): waste unit weight $\gamma_t = 112\ \mathrm{pcf}$, waste height $h = 30\ \mathrm{ft}$, clay (CH) $\gamma_{sat} = 131\ \mathrm{pcf}$, water table at the top of the clay.

## Given / Find

- Waste: $\gamma_t = 112\ \mathrm{pcf}$, $h = 30\ \mathrm{ft}$
- Clay (CH): $\gamma_{sat} = 131\ \mathrm{pcf}$, water table at the clay surface, $\gamma_w = 62.4\ \mathrm{pcf}$
- Point of interest: $z = 5\ \mathrm{ft}$ below the top of the clay
- **Find:** $\Delta\sigma_v$, $\sigma_{vf}$, $\sigma'_{vf}$ at $z = 5\ \mathrm{ft}$

## Assumptions

The landfill is much wider than the clay layer is thick, so it is treated as an infinitely loaded area and $\Delta\sigma_v = q$ at every depth.

The water table sits at the top of the clay, as drawn in the figure, so the clay is saturated from the surface down.

"Final" stresses are long-term values: the excess pore pressure generated by the landfill has dissipated, so the soil skeleton carries all of $\Delta\sigma_v$ and the pore pressure is back to hydrostatic.

The depth of 5 ft is measured from the original ground surface (top of the clay), not from the top of the waste.

## Solution

The in-situ stresses come from Module 3. Because the loaded area is effectively infinite, the increment of stress equals the surcharge at every depth, $\Delta\sigma_v = q$, and it adds directly to both the total and the effective in-situ stresses once consolidation is complete.

1. In-situ total stress — $\sigma_{v0} = \gamma_{sat}\, z = 131 \times 5 = 655\ \mathrm{psf}$
2. Pore pressure — $u_0 = \gamma_w\, z = 62.4 \times 5 = 312\ \mathrm{psf}$
3. In-situ effective stress — $\sigma'_{v0} = \sigma_{v0} - u_0 = 655 - 312 = 343\ \mathrm{psf}$
4. Surcharge (increment of stress) — $\Delta\sigma_v = q = \gamma_t\, h = 112 \times 30 = \mathbf{3360\ \mathrm{psf} = 3.36\ \mathrm{ksf}}$
5. Final total stress — $\sigma_{vf} = \sigma_{v0} + \Delta\sigma_v = 655 + 3360 = \mathbf{4015\ \mathrm{psf} \approx 4.02\ \mathrm{ksf}}$
6. Final effective stress — $\sigma'_{vf} = \sigma'_{v0} + \Delta\sigma_v = 343 + 3360 = \mathbf{3703\ \mathrm{psf} \approx 3.70\ \mathrm{ksf}}$

## Verification

```
sigma_v0=655.0 psf u=312.0 psf sigma'_v0=343.0 psf dsig=3360 psf
sigma_vf=4015.0 psf  sigma'_vf=3703.0 psf
```

Each value recomputed independently in Python. Units: pcf times ft gives psf on every line; $1\ \mathrm{ksf} = 1000\ \mathrm{psf}$. Magnitude check: 30 ft of waste is roughly five times the weight of the top 5 ft of clay, so $\Delta\sigma_v$ dominating the final stress is expected. The results agree with the handwritten solution (655, 343, 3360, 4015, 3703 psf).
