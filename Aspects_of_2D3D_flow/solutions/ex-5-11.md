## Statement

> A 10-m-thick layer of stiff saturated clay is underlain by a layer of sand. The sand is under artesian pressure. Calculate the maximum depth of cut, H, that can be made in the clay before the quicksand condition happens.

The sketch of the problem is `FiguresGeneral/EX511.png`, already on the problem slide, and it stays there. All data below are read from it.

## Given / Find

- Saturated clay: thickness $10\ \mathrm{m}$, $\gamma_{sat} = 19\ \mathrm{kN/m^3}$
- Sand: thickness $2.5\ \mathrm{m}$, $\gamma_{sat} = 18.1\ \mathrm{kN/m^3}$
- Piezometer in the sand: water rises $6\ \mathrm{m}$ above the clay–sand contact
- Point $A$: on the clay–sand contact, directly below the cut
- $\gamma_w = 9.81\ \mathrm{kN/m^3}$
- **Find:** $H_{max}$, the depth of cut at which $\sigma'_A = 0$

## Assumptions

The cut is kept dry, so the pressure head at its base is zero and no water load acts on the clay left beneath it.

The piezometric level in the sand is fixed by the artesian source and does not drop as the cut is deepened. The pore pressure at $A$ is therefore the same for every $H$.

The cut is wide enough that the clay plug beneath it gets no help from shear on its sides. Only its weight resists the uplift, so the check is a one-dimensional vertical balance at $A$.

The sand's unit weight and the $2.5\ \mathrm{m}$ thickness are not needed. Failure starts at the top of the sand, at $A$, so nothing below $A$ enters the calculation.

$\gamma_w = 9.81\ \mathrm{kN/m^3}$, the value used elsewhere in the course solutions.

## Solution

The quick condition occurs when the effective stress at the base of the clay drops to zero, $\sigma'_A = \sigma_A - u_A = 0$. The same limit can be reached through seepage, by setting the upward gradient through the clay plug equal to $i_{crit}$ so that $FS_p = 1$. Both routes are shown, as on the original slide, and both must give the same $H$.

**Data, find, principles** (part 1, beside the figure, as in the handwritten solution)

- Data: Figure
- Find: $H_{max}$
- Principles: $FS_p = \dfrac{i_{crit}}{i_{max}} = 1$, $i_{crit} = \dfrac{\gamma'}{\gamma_w}$, $i = \dfrac{\Delta h}{L}$

**Approach 1 — recall that the quick condition happens if $\sigma'_{vA} = 0$**

1. Pore pressure at $A$ — the piezometer stands $6\ \mathrm{m}$ above $A$, so $u_A = \gamma_w h_p = (9.81)(6) = 58.86\ \mathrm{kPa}$
2. Total stress at $A$ — only the clay left below the cut loads $A$: $\sigma_A = \gamma_{sat,clay}(10 - H) = 19(10 - H)\ \mathrm{kPa}$
3. Quick condition — $\sigma'_A = \sigma_A - u_A = 0 \Rightarrow 19(10 - H) = 58.86 \Rightarrow 10 - H = \dfrac{58.86}{19} = 3.10\ \mathrm{m}$
4. Maximum depth of cut — $H = \dfrac{10\,\gamma_{sat,clay} - 6\,\gamma_w}{\gamma_{sat,clay}} = \dfrac{10(19) - 6(9.81)}{19} = \mathbf{6.90\ \mathrm{m}}$

**Second approach — $i_{crit} = i_{max}$ because $FS_p = 1$**

5. Total heads, datum at $A$ — at $A$: $h_A = z_A + h_{p,A} = 0 + 6 = 6\ \mathrm{m}$; at the base of the cut, point $B$: $h_B = z_B + h_{p,B} = (10 - H) + 0 = 10 - H$
6. Gradient through the clay plug — flow is upward from $A$ to $B$ over $L = 10 - H$: $i_{max} = \dfrac{h_A - h_B}{L} = \dfrac{6 - (10 - H)}{10 - H} = \dfrac{H - 4}{10 - H}$
7. Critical gradient of the clay — $\gamma' = \gamma_{sat} - \gamma_w = 19 - 9.81 = 9.19\ \mathrm{kN/m^3}$, so $i_{crit} = \dfrac{\gamma'}{\gamma_w} = \dfrac{9.19}{9.81} = 0.937$
8. Solve $FS_p = 1$ — $i_{max} = i_{crit} \Rightarrow \dfrac{H - 4}{10 - H} = 0.937 \Rightarrow H - 4 = 0.937(10 - H) \Rightarrow H = \dfrac{4 + 9.37}{1.937} = \mathbf{6.90\ \mathrm{m}}$

## Verification

```
======== Example 5.11 - heave of a cut over artesian sand ========
  u_A = 6.0*9.81 = 58.8600 kPa
  sigma_A = (10-H)*19 ; sigma'_A=0 -> 10-H = 58.8600/19.0 = 3.0979 m ; H = 6.9021 m
  check: sigma_A = 58.8600 kPa, sigma'_A = 7.11e-15 kPa
  gamma' = 9.19 kN/m3 ; i_crit = 9.19/9.81 = 0.9368
  (H-4)/(10-H) = 0.9368 -> H = (4 + 0.9368*10)/(1+0.9368) = 13.3680/1.9368 = 6.9021 m
  at H=6.9021: h_A=6.0, h_B=3.0979, dh=2.9021, L=3.0979, i=0.9368
  H=6.0: sigma'_A = +17.140 kPa, FS=1.874
  H=6.5: sigma'_A = +7.640 kPa, FS=1.312
  H=7.0: sigma'_A = -1.860 kPa, FS=0.937
```

Every value was recomputed independently in Python. The two approaches were coded separately and both give $H = 6.9021\ \mathrm{m}$; this agreement is expected, because $i = i_{crit}$ is algebraically the same statement as $\sigma' = 0$ for vertical upward flow. Substituting $H = 6.90\ \mathrm{m}$ back gives $i_{max} = 0.9368 = i_{crit}$, which closes the check. Units: $u$ and $\sigma$ are in $\mathrm{kN/m^2} = \mathrm{kPa}$, the ratio $58.86/19$ is in metres, and both gradients are dimensionless. Magnitude: the last three lines show $FS_p$ falling through 1 between $H = 6.5$ and $7.0\ \mathrm{m}$, so 6.90 m sits where it should. The remaining 3.10 m of clay weighs $58.9\ \mathrm{kPa}$, which matches the 6 m of water pressure pushing up on it.
