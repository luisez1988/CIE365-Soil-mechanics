Particle size

Grain size influence the engineering behavior of soils.
Particles can be smaller than 2 \(\mu\)m or larger than 2m.
Water effects soil differently depending on texture. Fined-grained soils are more affected by water by affecting the interactions between mineral grains. We use two terms:
Ability to be molded
Ability to stick together

How do we characterize particle size gradation in soils?
In nature, soil particles are not perfectly uniform. To measure that variability we use:

  • Sieve analysis: For particles coarser than 0.075 mm
  • Hydrometer test: For the portion of soils finer than 0.075 mm (silts and clay)

The objective is to obtain the soil's grain size distribution (GSD).

Important sieve analysis definitions

Variable Definition Equation
Individual sieve weight \(W_i\) Weight of soil retained on sieve \(i\) -
Retained weight \(W_{ri}\) Cumulative weight of soil retained on sieve \(i\) \(W_{ri}=\sum_i W_i\)
Percentage retained \(P_{Ri}\) Percentage of mass retained on sieve \(i\) \(P_{Ri}=\frac{W_{ri}}{W_{td}}\times 100\)
Percentage passing \(P_{Pi}\) Percent soil mass passing sieve \(i\) \(P_{Pi}=100-P_{Ri}\)
Based on retained and passing percents we can define soil fractions
Variable Definition Equation
Gravel fraction \(G_F\) Cumulative retained percentage in Sieve No 4 or coarser than 4.75 mm \(G_F= P_{r,\#4}\)
Fine fraction \(F_F\) Portion of soils that passes Sieve No 200 or is finer than 0.075 mm \(F_F= P_{p,\#200}\)
Sand fraction \(S_F\) Percentage of soil that passes Sieve No 4 and is retained in Sieve No 200 \(S_F=100-G_F-F_F\)
Clay fraction \(C_F\) Portion of soils finer than 2 \(\mu\)m -

Notes on how draw/read GSD plots
  1. Must be plot in semi-log space. Grain sizes vary in orders of magnitude.
  2. x-axis (i.e., grain size) can be flipped. Double check x-axis direction before reading.
  3. Can use double-sided y-axis. Passing/finer and retained/coarser.
  4. Before reading the y-axis, double check that it is what you want (i.e., passing or retained).
  5. At a minimum, it should contain annotations and vertical lines on the size corresponding to sieve #4 and #200.

Hydrometer analysis

Complements the sieve analysis and extends the GSD beyond sieve No 200.
  • Segregation of particles by sedimentation (Stokes law).
  • Relative density of suspended flow is measured in time to determine particle diameter.
  • Assumes particles are spherical. Unrealistic assumption.

GSD metrics

Recognizable GSD features:

  • If the GSD is vertical , the soil is uniform , or poorly graded , or well sorted .
  • If the GSD smoothly spans across the sizes , the soil is well graded or poorly sorted .
  • If the GSD features horizontal segments, the soil is gap graded . Such portions indicate that no particles exist within that size range.

Coefficient of uniformity \(C_u\) Measures how uniform the soil is \(C_u=\cfrac{D_{60}}{D_{10}}\)

Interpretation of gradation coefficients

  • A higher \(C_u\) indicates a wider range of particle sizes, meaning the soil is less uniform.
  • \(C_c\) describes the shape of the GSD. \(C_c\) is also important for predicting flow patterns, pressure distribution, and other characteristics in curved channels
Gradation definitions for coarse-grained soils:
Gravel Well-graded \(C_u > 4\) & \( 1< C_c <3 \)
Uniform \(C_u \leq 4\)
Gap-graded \(C_c \leq 1\) or \( C_c > 3 \)
Sand Well-graded \(C_u > 6\) & \( 1< C_c <3 \)
Uniform \(C_u \leq 6\)
Gap-graded \(C_c \leq 1\) or \( C_c > 3 \)
