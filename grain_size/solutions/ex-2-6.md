## Statement

## Given / Find

- The GSD curve of `EX270.svg`, which is the Example 2.5 soil: $P_P = 97.55,\ 51.72,\ 23.45,\ 8.59,\ 3.60,\ 1.61,\ 0.53,\ 0.20\ \%$ at $d = 4.75,\ 2,\ 0.85,\ 0.425,\ 0.25,\ 0.15,\ 0.106,\ 0.075\ \mathrm{mm}$

- **Find:** $D_{50}$, $C_u$, $C_c$

## Assumptions

**Every $D_x$ is read off the curve, not interpolated from a table.** This is the route the slide asks for and the only one available to a student looking at the figure. All four readings, and both coefficients derived from them, are reported to **three significant figures** — which is as much as a log axis supports by eye and no more.

**The curve is the GSD of the Example 2.5 soil.** Established, not assumed, in `ex-2-5.md`: the eight markers in `EX270.svg` decode through the figure's own axis calibration to the eight sieve points of Example 2.5, agreeing to three decimal places on both coordinates. It matters in step 6, where the classification threshold depends on whether the coarse fraction is sand or gravel — information the student has from the previous example.

**The coarse fraction is sand, not gravel.** Example 2.5 gives $S_F = 97.35\ \%$ against $G_F = 2.45\ \%$. This matters only in step 6: the deck's gradation table sets the uniform/well-graded threshold at $C_u \le 6$ for sand but $C_u \le 4$ for gravel, and this soil's $C_u$ falls between those two numbers.

## Solution

$D_x$ is defined on the deck's metrics slide as the size for which $x$ percent of the soil is **finer**, so every reading is taken off the percent-passing axis. The whole example is four readings, two ratios and one lookup — the only real decisions are which axis to enter the graph on, and which of the two gradation limits applies once $C_u$ is known.

1. Enter the graph on the correct axis — $D_x$ is defined on *percent finer*, so all four readings are taken from the passing axis, not the retained axis; `EX270.svg` carries both, which is exactly the trap the deck's "notes on how to read GSD plots" slide warns about
2. Read the four sizes off the curve — at $10\ \%$, $30\ \%$, $50\ \%$ and $60\ \%$ finer: $D_{10} = 0.440\ \mathrm{mm}$, $D_{30} = 1.00\ \mathrm{mm}$, $D_{50} = 1.90\ \mathrm{mm}$, $D_{60} = 2.20\ \mathrm{mm}$
3. Median grain size — $D_{50}$ is read directly, no formula: $D_{50} = \mathbf{1.90\ \mathrm{mm}}$
4. Coefficient of uniformity — $C_u = \dfrac{D_{60}}{D_{10}} = \dfrac{2.20}{0.440} = \mathbf{5.00}$
5. Coefficient of curvature — $C_c = \dfrac{D_{30}^2}{D_{10}\,D_{60}} = \dfrac{(1.00)^2}{0.440 \times 2.20} = \dfrac{1.00}{0.968} = \mathbf{1.03}$
6. Classify the gradation — the soil is a sand ($S_F = 97.35\ \%$ from Example 2.5), so the sand limits apply: $C_u = 5.00 \le 6$, therefore the soil is **uniform** (equivalently poorly graded, or well sorted)
7. Validate — the four readings increase with $x$ as they must ($0.440 < 1.00 < 1.90 < 2.20\ \mathrm{mm}$), $C_u = 5.00 \ge 1$ as it must be for any real soil, and $C_c \approx 1$ says the curve has no shoulder between $D_{10}$ and $D_{60}$ — all three agree with the single smooth inflection visible in the figure and with the $97\ \%$ sand fraction from Example 2.5

## Verification

The interpolation check below stays in this record and is **not** shown on the slide — per your answer under Flags, the slide reports the graph reads only. It is here because the graph reads are claims and something has to test them.

```python
import math

def logterp(x, di, Pi, dj, Pj):
    """Deck formula: log(Dx/di) = log(di/dj)/(Pi-Pj) * (x-Pi).  i = coarser sieve."""
    return di * 10 ** (math.log10(di / dj) / (Pi - Pj) * (x - Pi))

# Example 2.5 table, recomputed from the raw sieve weights (not transcribed)
pts = [(4.75, 97.548), (2.0, 51.718), (0.85, 23.450), (0.425, 8.593),
       (0.25, 3.597), (0.15, 1.608), (0.106, 0.530), (0.075, 0.196)]
D = {}
for x in (10, 30, 50, 60):
    for (di, Pi), (dj, Pj) in zip(pts, pts[1:]):
        if Pj <= x <= Pi:
            D[x] = logterp(x, di, Pi, dj, Pj)
            break
Cu = D[60] / D[10]
Cc = D[30] ** 2 / (D[10] * D[60])

# the graph reads reported on the slide, and the two error modes of step 5
g10, g30, g50, g60 = 0.440, 1.00, 1.90, 2.20
g_Cu, g_Cc = g60 / g10, g30 ** 2 / (g10 * g60)
bad_Cu, bad_Cc = g10 / g60, g30 ** 2 / (g10 + g60)
```

The four reported readings were tested against exact logarithmic interpolation on the Example 2.5 sieve table, which is an independent route: it never touches the figure. Every reading agrees to better than $6\ \%$ — $0.1\ \%$ on $D_{50}$, $3.0\ \%$ on $D_{10}$, $3.5\ \%$ on $D_{30}$ and $5.9\ \%$ on $D_{60}$, the last being the largest because $D_{60}$ sits on a long, steep segment between the 2 mm and 4.75 mm sieves. The two coefficients differ by $3\ \%$ and $2\ \%$ respectively ($C_u = 5.00$ against $5.15$, $C_c = 1.03$ against $1.01$), and the classification in step 6 is the same either way — which is what licenses reporting the graph reads as the answers. The interpolation formula itself was checked at both endpoints: substituting $x = P_{P,i}$ returns $d_i$ and $x = P_{P,i+1}$ returns $d_{i+1}$, to six decimal places. Units: every $D$ is in millimetres and both coefficients are dimensionless, $C_u$ as a ratio of two lengths and $C_c$ as length² over length². Magnitude check: $D_{50} = 1.90\ \mathrm{mm}$ is coarse sand and $C_u = 5.00$ is a narrow size range, both consistent with the $97\ \%$ sand fraction carried over from Example 2.5.

## Scaffolding decisions

Step 1 obtains no number and has no blank. It exists because the single most common error in this example is reading $D_{10}$ off the *retained* axis and getting $D_{90}$ — and `EX270.svg` has both axes drawn, labelled "Percent finer" on the left and "Percent coarser" on the right. Making the axis choice a numbered step, before any reading happens, is cheaper than a note attached to step 2.

Step 2 is one step producing four numbers, because it is one physical act: reading the curve. The four values are blue in a small `.sol-table` with $x$ pre-printed in black and $D_x$ blank, so the student has to enter the graph four times rather than copy a list. Reading $D_{60}$ is deliberately included even though the problem never asks for it — it is an input to both coefficients, and students who skip it tend to reach for $D_{50}$ instead. All four cells share `data-step="2"` so the row reveals as one click rather than four.

Step 3 is a separate step from step 2 even though it is a transcription, because $D_{50}$ is a requested answer and must be green. It is the one place in the example where the answer is obtained without any arithmetic at all, and saying so out loud is worth a step.

Steps 4 and 5 pre-print both definitions in black — they are on the deck's metrics slide two slides earlier, so recalling them is not the exercise. The blanks are on the substitutions, so the student has to place $D_{60}$ in the numerator and $D_{30}^2$ over the *product* $D_{10}D_{60}$. Blanking only the results would not distinguish the two kinds of error. An inverted $C_u$ gives $D_{10}/D_{60} = 0.200$, which is visibly impossible because $C_u \ge 1$ by construction and the student catches it unaided. But writing $C_c = D_{30}^2/(D_{10} + D_{60})$ — sum instead of product, the commonest slip in this formula — gives $0.379$, which is not visibly impossible and would send the soil to "gap graded" in step 6. Only a blank on the substitution catches that one.

Step 6 is the only step whose answer is a word. $C_u = 5.00$ sits between the gravel threshold (4) and the sand threshold (6), so the classification cannot be made without first knowing the soil is a sand — which comes from Example 2.5, not from this slide. That dependency is the whole content of the step, and it is why the fractions appear under Given. Both the threshold used and the verdict are green.

Step 7 is the validation, and it deliberately contains **no interpolation** — per your answer under Flags, the slide never shows a second set of numbers. It validates on internal consistency instead: the readings must increase with $x$, $C_u$ must be at least 1, and $C_c \approx 1$ must match a curve with no shoulder. Two blue values, the rest black.

Red notes, at most one per step: (1) both axes are printed on this figure and they run in opposite directions — check which one before reading; (2) on a log axis, halfway between 1 and 10 is about 3, not 5.5, so interpolate by eye against the minor gridlines and not linearly; (4) $C_u$ is $D_{60}$ over $D_{10}$ — the *larger* size on top, so $C_u \ge 1$ always, and $C_u = 1$ is a single grain size; (6) the threshold is 6 for sand but 4 for gravel, so identify the coarse fraction before quoting a limit.

The step-6 note exceeds the budget and carries a `data-slide` short form: "Threshold is $C_u\le6$ for sand, $C_u\le4$ for gravel — check the fractions first."

No `data-part` split. The slide is a two-column layout with the figure at 60 % and a `ProblemBox` at 40 %; with step 7 shortened to an internal-consistency check, seven steps fit in one column.
