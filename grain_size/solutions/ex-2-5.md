## Statement

> A sieve analysis was conducted on a soil producing the data shown in the table below. **Calculate (i) the retained weights, (ii) Percentage retained, (iii) Percentage passing, (iv) the gravel, sand, and fine fractions, (v) and plot the grain size distribution.**

Self-contained. The sieve openings and the individual sieve weights $W_i$ are both in the HTML table on the slide, and the stated total is given so the sum can be checked rather than assumed.

## Given / Find

| Sieve opening (mm) | 4.75 | 2 | 0.85 | 0.425 | 0.25 | 0.15 | 0.106 | 0.075 | Pan | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $W_i$ (g) | 12.5 | 233.67 | 144.13 | 75.75 | 25.47 | 10.14 | 5.5 | 1.7 | 1 | 509.86 |

- **Find:** $W_{ri}$, $P_{Ri}$, $P_{Pi}$ for every sieve; then $G_F$, $S_F$, $F_F$; then the GSD plot.

## Assumptions

**The weights in the table are the *individual* sieve weights $W_i$, not already-cumulated ones.** The column is headed "Weight", which is ambiguous against the deck's own definitions slide, where $W_i$ is individual and $W_{ri}$ is cumulative. Two facts settle it: the listed total 509.86 g equals $\sum W_i$ exactly, which would be meaningless if the column were already cumulative; and the values fall and then rise again ($12.5 \to 233.67 \to 144.13$), which a cumulative column cannot do. This reading is required for the problem to have an answer, so it is stated on the slide as a red note.

**The sample is oven-dry, so $W_{td} = 509.86\ \mathrm{g}$ is a dry weight.** The deck defines $P_{Ri} = W_{ri}/W_{td} \times 100$ with $W_{td}$ the *total dry weight*; ASTM D6913 washes and oven-dries before sieving. Nothing in the arithmetic changes, but the symbol would be wrong otherwise.

**The pan catch is soil finer than the 0.075 mm (No. 200) sieve**, i.e. it is fines, not spillage. This is what makes $F_F$ equal to the percentage passing the No. 200 sieve.

**No particles are lost during sieving.** $\sum W_i$ reproduces the stated total to the last digit, so this is verified rather than assumed here — but it is the check that licenses $P_{Ri}$ reaching exactly 100 % at the pan.

## Solution

Nothing in this example needs a formula beyond the three definitions on the deck's "Important sieve analysis definitions" slide. The whole problem is one running sum and two divisions repeated down a table, and the only place a decision is made is at the two boundary sieves — No. 4 and No. 200 — where the table turns into the soil fractions.

1. Total dry weight — $W_{td} = \sum_i W_i = 12.5 + 233.67 + 144.13 + 75.75 + 25.47 + 10.14 + 5.5 + 1.7 + 1 = 509.86\ \mathrm{g}$, which matches the stated total, so nothing was lost
2. Work one row, the 2 mm sieve — retained weight is cumulative down the stack, $W_{ri} = \sum_{k=1}^{i} W_k = 12.5 + 233.67 = 246.17\ \mathrm{g}$; then $P_{Ri} = \dfrac{W_{ri}}{W_{td}} \times 100 = \dfrac{246.17}{509.86} \times 100 = 48.28\ \%$; then $P_{Pi} = 100 - P_{Ri} = 100 - 48.28 = 51.72\ \%$
3. Repeat for every sieve — the completed table, which is the answer to (i), (ii) and (iii):

| Opening (mm) | $W_i$ (g) | $W_{ri}$ (g) | $P_{Ri}$ (%) | $P_{Pi}$ (%) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.75 (No. 4) | 12.5 | 12.50 | 2.45 | 97.55 |
| 2 (No. 10) | 233.67 | 246.17 | 48.28 | 51.72 |
| 0.85 (No. 20) | 144.13 | 390.30 | 76.55 | 23.45 |
| 0.425 (No. 40) | 75.75 | 466.05 | 91.41 | 8.59 |
| 0.25 (No. 60) | 25.47 | 491.52 | 96.40 | 3.60 |
| 0.15 (No. 100) | 10.14 | 501.66 | 98.39 | 1.61 |
| 0.106 (No. 140) | 5.5 | 507.16 | 99.47 | 0.53 |
| 0.075 (No. 200) | 1.7 | 508.86 | 99.80 | 0.20 |
| Pan | 1 | 509.86 | 100.00 | 0.00 |

4. Gravel fraction — everything coarser than 4.75 mm, which is the cumulative percentage *retained* on the No. 4 sieve: $G_F = P_{R,\#4} = \mathbf{2.45\ \%}$
5. Fine fraction — everything finer than 0.075 mm, which is the percentage *passing* the No. 200 sieve: $F_F = P_{P,\#200} = \mathbf{0.20\ \%}$
6. Sand fraction — what is left, $S_F = 100 - G_F - F_F = 100 - 2.45 - 0.20 = \mathbf{97.35\ \%}$
7. Plot the GSD — $P_{Pi}$ on a linear vertical axis against opening on a **logarithmic** horizontal axis, one point per sieve, with vertical reference lines drawn at 4.75 mm and 0.075 mm; this is `FiguresGeneral/EX270.svg`
8. Validate — the pan row closes at exactly $P_R = 100\ \%$ and $P_P = 0\ \%$, the three fractions sum to $2.45 + 97.35 + 0.20 = 100\ \%$, and the pan weight expressed as a percentage, $1/509.86 \times 100 = 0.196\ \%$, equals $F_F$ independently; with $97\ \%$ of the mass between the No. 4 and No. 200 sieves this is a clean sand

All sixteen decoded quantities reproduce the computed table to three decimal places, which simultaneously verifies the arithmetic and establishes that `EX270.svg` is a plot of *this* soil — which matters because Example 2.6 reads its four $D_x$ values off that same curve. Magnitude check: $97.35\ \%$ sand with $0.20\ \%$ fines and a trace of gravel is a clean, essentially fines-free sand, which is consistent with a laboratory teaching sample and with the smooth single-inflection curve in `EX270.svg`.

## Scaffolding decisions

Step 1 is the running total, and it is deliberately its own step rather than a footnote to the table. The statement hands over the answer (509.86 g), so the blue blank is on the *sum*, not on the given — the student adds nine numbers and discovers they match. That is the only "no particles were lost" check available, and skipping it is how a mis-keyed weight propagates silently through all three columns.

Step 2 is **one sample calculation carried across all three columns** on a single sieve — the 2 mm row — with each governing equation pre-printed in black and the substitution and result in blue. The three columns are one step rather than three because they are one physical act: working a row. The six blue values are grouped into three reveals with `data-step`, so each column appears as a unit. This is the split the rule demands: choosing *which* weights go into a cumulative sum, and choosing $W_{td}$ rather than $W_i$ as the denominator, are decisions; grinding the same three operations down eight more rows is bookkeeping.

Step 3 is the completed `.sol-table`, and blanks in it are restricted to three rows: 4.75 mm, because it is the first row and shows that the first cumulative weight *is* the first individual weight — and because $P_{R,\#4}$ is consumed by step 4; 0.075 mm, because $P_{P,\#200}$ is consumed by step 5; and the Pan row, because it must close at exactly 100.00 and 0.00 and that closure is the validation. Nine blanks, grouped by `data-step` into three reveals, one per row. The 2 mm row is pre-printed because step 2 just computed it, and the five middle rows are pre-printed because they are pure repetition — blanking all 27 cells would put the slide past forty reveal clicks with no pedagogic return.

Steps 4, 5 and 6 are three separate steps even though each is one line, because each is a different *decision* about which column of the table to read. The classic error is symmetrical and worth separating: $G_F$ is read from the **retained** column and $F_F$ from the **passing** column, and students who read both from the same column get a plausible-looking wrong answer. All three fractions are green — the problem asked for them.

Step 7 carries no arithmetic and no blank. It is a `.sol-fig` holding `EX270.svg` with the plotting rules restated in black: log axis, percent finer, reference lines at the No. 4 and No. 200 openings. Everything on that list is reading, not deciding. The figure is the *answer* to part (v) — the student draws the curve and this is what it should look like.

Step 8 is black except for the two sums, which are blue.

Red notes, at most one per step: (1) the "Weight" column is $W_i$, individual — not already cumulative (this is the Assumptions entry, and it must appear on the slide); (2) cumulate *downwards*, coarse to fine, and the denominator is always $W_{td}$, never the sieve's own $W_i$; (4) gravel comes from the **retained** column and fines from the **passing** column — that contrast note is placed once, at step 4, rather than repeated at step 5; (7) a linear size axis is the single most common plotting error, and it hides the fine end of the curve entirely.

The step-1 note is the longest and carries a `data-slide` short form, because the full justification (the total matches, and the values rise and fall) runs past the 110-character budget.

The solution is split with `data-part`. The example occupies one slide whose solution column is only 60 % wide, and the table plus eight steps will not fit. Part 1 = steps 1–3, ending with the completed table; part 2 = steps 4–8. A second slide was added in Phase 2.
