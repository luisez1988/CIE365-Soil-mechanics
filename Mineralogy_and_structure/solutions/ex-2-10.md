## Statement

> The plot below shows the full grain size distribution of a soil with \(PL=12\%\) and \(LL=43\%\). Estimate: (a) the clay fraction of the soil, (b) the activity of the soil, (c) the swelling potential of the soil, and (d) asses the likely mineral content in this soil.

Figure-dependent. The clay fraction can only come off `EX211.svg`, so the curve was decoded from the SVG rather than read by eye — see Verification. The other two figures the example needs are already on the deck's own slides: the Seed et al. (1962) swelling-potential chart (`correlations.svg`, used on the "Swelling potential is strongly correlated to activity" slide) and the mineralogy plasticity chart (`plasticity_chart.jpg`, used on the "How can we assess the mineral content of a clay?" slide).

## Given / Find

- $PL = 12\ \%$, $LL = 43\ \%$, and the full grain size distribution in `EX211.svg`
- **Find:** (a) $C_F$, (b) $A$, (c) $S_P$ class, (d) the dominant clay mineral

## Assumptions

**"Clay fraction" means the percentage by mass finer than $0.002\ \mathrm{mm}$, a *size* class, not a mineralogical one.** This is the definition the deck's own `defBox` gives — "the percentage of clay-sized particles ($<0.002$ mm)" — and it is the reading used throughout. It matters because part (d) then asks what mineral those particles are, and the two questions are independent: a soil can be $20\ \%$ clay-size and contain no clay mineral at all. Recorded because conflating the two is the standard confusion in this topic, and it becomes a red note.

**The GSD's $y$ axis is percent *finer*, so $C_F$ is read directly at $0.002\ \mathrm{mm}$ with no subtraction.** The figure labels both axes — "Percent finer" on the left, "Percent coarser" on the right — so this is a reading rather than a choice, but it is where marks are lost, and the right-hand axis is there precisely to catch people out.

**$C_F$ is taken as $20\ \%$.** Decoding the curve gives $20.38\ \%$ at $0.002\ \mathrm{mm}$, and the figure's own construction line is drawn at exactly $20.0\ \%$ with the label $C_F = 20\ \%$. Two significant figures is the resolution a student can actually read off a printed log axis, so $20\ \%$ is the value carried forward. The $0.38\ \%$ difference moves $A$ from $1.55$ to $1.52$ and changes nothing downstream.

**The swelling potential is reported as the chart's *class*, not as a single number.** The chart at `correlations.svg` is a zoned chart bounded by the contours $S_P = 1.5\ \%$, $5\ \%$ and $25\ \%$; a point between two contours gives a band, not a value. The answer is therefore "$1.5\ \% < S_P < 5\ \%$, i.e. Medium", which is what the statement's "estimate" invites.

**The soil is inorganic and the limits are a valid pair.** $PI = 31\ \%$ against a U-line value of $31.5\ \%$ at $LL = 43\ \%$ means the limits sit just inside the physically attainable region, so no test error is indicated. Nothing in the statement suggests organics, and the mineralogy chart has no organic field, so the question is not raised on the slide — recorded here for completeness.

## Solution

Activity is the bridge: it converts two numbers a laboratory measures on the whole soil — the Atterberg limits — into a statement about the *mineral* carrying the plasticity, by dividing out how much clay-size material there is. Parts (a) and (b) build that number, and parts (c) and (d) are two independent lookups that use it, each on its own chart, which is why they should agree.

1. Clay fraction — the clay-size boundary is $d = 0.002\ \mathrm{mm}$; entering the GSD there and reading down to the "percent finer" axis gives $C_F = \mathbf{20\ \%}$
2. Plasticity index — $PI = LL - PL = 43 - 12 = 31\ \%$
3. Activity — $A = \dfrac{PI}{C_F} = \dfrac{31}{20} = \mathbf{1.55}$, and $1.55 > 1.25$, so the soil is **active**
4. Swelling potential — entering the Seed et al. (1962) chart at $C_F = 20\ \%$ and $A = 1.55$ lands between the $S_P = 1.5\ \%$ contour (at $A = 1.28$ here) and the $S_P = 5\ \%$ contour (at $A = 1.98$), so $1.5\ \% < S_P < 5\ \%$ — a **Medium** swelling potential
5. Position on the plasticity chart — the A-line is $PI_A = 0.73(LL-20) = 0.73(43-20) = 16.8$ and the U-line is $PI_U = 0.9(LL-8) = 0.9(43-8) = 31.5$; with $PI = 31$ the point sits $14.2$ above the A-line but only $0.5$ **below the U-line**
6. Dominant mineral — a point hugging the U-line falls in the montmorillonite band, so the soil is dominated by **montmorillonite**; the activity agrees independently, since $A = 1.55$ matches Ca-montmorillonite ($\approx 1.5$) and is above illite's ceiling of $1.3$

The two lookups are worth naming as a pair when the example is presented: part (c) says the soil will swell moderately, part (d) says why — a small amount of a very active mineral.

import math

# ---------------------------------------------------------------- given
LL, PL = 43.0, 12.0

# ------------------------------------------- 1. read C_F off EX211.svg
# The figure is matplotlib output re-saved by Inkscape, so the axes survive as
# tick <use> elements and the curve as one cubic Bezier. Both are decoded here;
# nothing is read off a picture by eye.
#   x: log10(d) = +2 at 28.508724, -4 at 253.88414   (outer SVG units)
#   y: 0 % finer at 149.28413,   100 % finer at 2.9112773
XL, XR = 28.508724, 253.88414
YB, YT = 149.28413, 2.9112773
x_of_d = lambda d: XL + (2 - math.log10(d))/6*(XR - XL)
d_of_x = lambda x: 10**(2 - (x - XL)/(XR - XL)*6)
p_of_y = lambda y: (YB - y)/(YB - YT)*100

# curve, path id="path4":  m 69.711484,2.9112786 c 121.584986,0  85.049716,114.1578714  166.520026,131.4659714
P0 = (69.711484, 2.9112786)
P = [P0] + [(P0[0]+dx, P0[1]+dy) for dx, dy in
            [(121.584986, 0), (85.049716, 114.1578714), (166.520026, 131.4659714)]]
bez = lambda t, i: ((1-t)**3*P[0][i] + 3*(1-t)**2*t*P[1][i]
                    + 3*(1-t)*t**2*P[2][i] + t**3*P[3][i])

def pct_finer(d):
    xt = x_of_d(d)
    if not bez(0, 0) <= xt <= bez(1, 0):
        return None
    lo, hi = 0.0, 1.0
    for _ in range(200):
        m = (lo+hi)/2
        lo, hi = (m, hi) if bez(m, 0) < xt else (lo, m)
    return p_of_y(bez((lo+hi)/2, 1))

print("EX211.svg decoded -- axis calibration checked against the figure's own annotations")
for x_inner, want in [(127.8405, 4.75), (228.37158, 0.075)]:            # the two red sieve lines
    got = d_of_x(0.67316434*x_inner - 7.8421502)                        # inner -> outer transform
    print("   red dashed line at x=%9.4f  ->  d = %8.4f mm   (drawn as sieve %s)  err %.2f %%"
          % (x_inner, got, "No. 4" if want > 1 else "No. 200", abs(got-want)/want*100))
print("   curve runs from d = %.2f mm at %.1f %% finer  to  d = %.5f mm at %.1f %% finer"
      % (d_of_x(bez(0, 0)), p_of_y(bez(0, 1)), d_of_x(bez(1, 0)), p_of_y(bez(1, 1))))
print()
print("percent finer, from the curve:")
for d in [4.75, 2.0, 0.425, 0.075, 0.05, 0.005, 0.002, 0.001]:
    print("   d = %8.4f mm  ->  %6.2f %% finer%s"
          % (d, pct_finer(d), "   <- clay-size boundary" if d == 0.002 else ""))

# the figure's own green construction line, path id="path5":
#   M 205.01411,149.28413 V 120.00956 H 28.508725
gx, gy = 205.01411, 120.00956
print("   figure's green construction line: vertical at d = %.5f mm, horizontal at %.2f %% finer"
      % (d_of_x(gx), p_of_y(gy)))
print("   -> the drop line IS at the 0.002 mm clay boundary (err %.3f %%); label reads C_F = 20 %%"
      % (abs(d_of_x(gx)-0.002)/0.002*100))
CF = 20.0
print("   curve reads %.2f %% there; the annotation rounds to %.0f %%  -> C_F = %.0f %%"
      % (pct_finer(0.002), CF, CF))
print()

# ------------------------------------------------ 2-3. PI and activity
PI = LL - PL
A = PI/CF
band = "inactive" if A < 0.75 else ("normal" if A <= 1.25 else "ACTIVE")
print("PI = LL - PL = %.0f - %.0f = %.0f %%" % (LL, PL, PI))
print("A  = PI/C_F  = %.0f / %.0f = %.4f  -> %.2f   (%s: deck bands <0.75 | 0.75-1.25 | >1.25)"
      % (PI, CF, A, round(A, 2), band))
print()

# ------------------------- 4. swelling potential, Seed et al. (1962) chart
# correlations.svg decoded the same way: x = C_F [%], y = activity, three Bezier
# boundaries at S_P = 1.5 %, 5 % and 25 %.
X0, SX = 197.6259, 1.597989          # x_svg = X0 + C_F*SX
Y0, SY = 45.30666, 18.654733         # y_svg = Y0 - A*SY
xf, af = lambda cf: X0 + cf*SX, lambda y: (Y0 - y)/SY
cff = lambda x: (x - X0)/SX
rel = lambda p, *ds: [p] + [(p[0]+dx, p[1]+dy) for dx, dy in ds]
bz = lambda Q, t, i: ((1-t)**3*Q[0][i] + 3*(1-t)**2*t*Q[1][i]
                      + 3*(1-t)*t**2*Q[2][i] + t**3*Q[3][i])
bnd = {                                                    # S_P contour -> Bezier segments
 1.5: [rel((209.79357, -48.456171), (2.85292, 43.2703383), (4.56103, 80.233773), (62.25353, 87.187174)),
       rel((272.0471, 38.731003), (10.56584, 1.273449), (22.89206, 2.060709), (37.1704, 2.496304))],
 5.0: [rel((215.09427, -48.319471), (3.70232, 62.450848), (23.82958, 83.925138), (94.69676, 87.257298))],
 25.0: [[(223.06414, -48.56137), (234.8098, 8.7573363), (254.31851, 23.6371), (309.4638, 33.068188)]],
}

def A_boundary(segs, cf):
    xt = xf(cf)
    for Q in segs:
        if bz(Q, 0, 0) - 1e-9 <= xt <= bz(Q, 1, 0) + 1e-9:
            lo, hi = 0.0, 1.0
            for _ in range(200):
                m = (lo+hi)/2
                lo, hi = (m, hi) if bz(Q, m, 0) < xt else (lo, m)
            return af(bz(Q, (lo+hi)/2, 1))
    return None

print("correlations.svg (Seed et al. 1962) decoded -- axes 0-100 % C_F, 0-5 activity")
for sp_, segs in sorted(bnd.items()):
    s, e = (bz(segs[0], 0, 0), bz(segs[0], 0, 1)), (bz(segs[-1], 1, 0), bz(segs[-1], 1, 1))
    print("   S_P = %4.1f %% contour spans C_F %.1f..%.1f %%,  A %.2f..%.3f"
          % (sp_, cff(s[0]), cff(e[0]), af(s[1]), af(e[1])))
b = {k: A_boundary(v, CF) for k, v in bnd.items()}
print("   at C_F = %.0f %%:  S_P=1.5 %% contour at A = %.3f | S_P=5 %% at A = %.3f | S_P=25 %% at A = %.3f"
      % (CF, b[1.5], b[5.0], b[25.0]))
zone = ("Low" if A < b[1.5] else "MEDIUM" if A < b[5.0] else "High" if A < b[25.0] else "Very high")
print("   soil at A = %.2f  ->  %s  (1.5 %% < S_P < 5 %%)" % (A, zone))
print("   margin to the Low/Medium contour: %+.3f in activity (%.0f %% of the way to the 5 %% contour)"
      % (A - b[1.5], (A - b[1.5])/(b[5.0] - b[1.5])*100))
print()

# ---------------------------- 5-6. plasticity chart, mineral identification
PI_A = 0.73*(LL - 20)
PI_U = 0.90*(LL - 8)
print("plasticity chart (Mitchell & Soga): A-line PI = 0.73(LL-20), U-line PI = 0.9(LL-8)")
print("   A-line at LL = %.0f  ->  PI_A = %.2f      PI - PI_A = %+.2f  (above the A-line)" % (LL, PI_A, PI - PI_A))
print("   U-line at LL = %.0f  ->  PI_U = %.2f      PI - PI_U = %+.2f  (just BELOW the U-line)" % (LL, PI_U, PI - PI_U))
print("   deck's own rule: montmorillonites just below the U-line | illites just above the A-line |")
print("                    kaolinites just below the A-line   ->  MONTMORILLONITE")
print("   cross-check on activity, deck's table:  Ca-montmorillonite 1.5, illite 0.5-1.3, kaolinite 0.3-0.5")
print("      A = %.2f matches Ca-montmorillonite (1.5) and exceeds illite's ceiling -> same verdict" % A)
print()
print("   NOTE the deck's present text says 'plots just above the A-line => Montmorillonite'.")
print("   By the deck's own rule 'just above the A-line' is ILLITE. The point is %.2f above the" % (PI - PI_A))
print("   A-line and only %.2f below the U-line, i.e. it is a U-line read. Answer right, reason wrong."
      % abs(PI - PI_U))
print()

# --------------------------------------------------------- consistency
print("consistency checks")
print("   PL < LL:", PL < LL, " PI > 0:", PI > 0, " PI <= PI_U (limits physically possible):", PI <= PI_U)
print("   C_F = %.0f %% <= fines fraction %.1f %% (percent finer at the No. 200, 0.075 mm):" % (CF, pct_finer(0.075)),
      CF <= pct_finer(0.075))
print("   units: C_F and PI are percentages; A = PI/C_F is dimensionless; S_P is a percentage volume change")
print("   magnitude: 20 % clay carrying PI = 31 % means each unit of clay does a lot of work --")
print("              that is what an activity above 1.25 means, and it points at a smectite.")

Nothing here was read off a picture. `EX211.svg` is matplotlib output re-saved through Inkscape, so its axes survive as tick `<use>` elements and its curve as a single cubic Bézier; both were decoded from the file, and the curve was then evaluated numerically at $0.002\ \mathrm{mm}$. The axis calibration was validated before it was used, against two independent marks the figure draws for its own purposes: the red dashed sieve lines come back as $4.7500$ and $0.0750\ \mathrm{mm}$, the ASTM No. 4 and No. 200 openings, to better than $0.005\ \%$. That is the check that the log axis was mapped correctly, and it is worth more than any reading of the curve.

The clay fraction was then confirmed twice over. The decoded curve gives $20.38\ \%$ finer at $0.002\ \mathrm{mm}$; the figure's own green construction line is drawn vertically at $0.00200\ \mathrm{mm}$ — the clay boundary to three decimals, not an approximation of it — and horizontally at exactly $20.00\ \%$. So the drop line is at the right diameter and the printed label $C_F = 20\ \%$ is a faithful rounding of the curve, $0.38\ \%$ below what the curve strictly reads. Carrying $20.38\ \%$ instead would give $A = 1.52$ rather than $1.55$: same activity class, same swelling class, same mineral. The example is not sensitive to that digit.

Activity, plasticity index and both chart lookups were computed rather than transcribed. The Seed et al. (1962) chart in `correlations.svg` was decoded by the same method as the GSD — its three zone boundaries are Bézier curves and its axes are $0\text{–}100\ \%$ clay fraction against $0\text{–}5$ activity — and evaluating them at $C_F = 20\ \%$ puts the $S_P = 1.5\ \%$ contour at $A = 1.28$ and the $S_P = 5\ \%$ contour at $A = 1.98$. The soil's $A = 1.55$ sits $38\ \%$ of the way across that band, comfortably clear of both edges, so the Medium verdict does not depend on a careful read.

The mineral identification was cross-checked two ways and they agree. On the plasticity chart the point $(LL, PI) = (43, 31)$ is $0.5$ below the U-line and $14.2$ above the A-line, i.e. it hugs the U-line, which is the montmorillonite band; on the activity table $A = 1.55$ matches Ca-montmorillonite at $1.5$ and lies above illite's ceiling of $1.3$. Two independent routes, one verdict.

Units and magnitudes: $C_F$, $PI$, $LL$ and $PL$ are all percentages, $A = PI/C_F$ is dimensionless, and $S_P$ is a percentage volume change — no conversion appears anywhere in the example. Internal consistency holds: $PL < LL$, $PI > 0$, and $PI = 31 \le PI_U = 31.5$, so the limits are physically attainable and neither is a typo. $C_F = 20\ \%$ is properly less than the $81.5\ \%$ of the soil finer than the No. 200, so the clay fraction is a subset of the fines, as it must be. And the magnitude tells the story the example exists for: only a fifth of this soil is clay-size, yet it carries $PI = 31\ \%$, which is a lot of plasticity from a little clay — the definition of an active mineral.

**One correction to the deck's present answer.** The existing slide reads "From the plasticity chart, the soil plots just above the A-line $\implies$ Montmorillonite". By the deck's own rule, stated three slides earlier, "just above the A-line" is the **illite** criterion; montmorillonite is "just below the U-line". The numbers say the point is $14.2$ above the A-line and $0.5$ below the U-line, so it is unambiguously a U-line read. The answer montmorillonite is right; the reason given for it is wrong, and as written it teaches the wrong rule. Step 5 in the solution above fixes this.

## Scaffolding decisions

**Six steps across two parts, matching the deck's two slides.** `data-part="1"` on steps 1–3 (parts a and b of the statement), `data-part="2"` on steps 4–6 (parts c and d). Step numbering continues across the parts, so part 2 opens at step 4.

**Part 2 keeps the existing two-column figure layout.** The deck's second slide puts `correlations.svg` and `plasticity_chart.jpg` side by side in two bordered `.col` divs inside one `ProblemBox`. That maps onto `.sol-cols` with the swelling chart and step 4 in the left `.sol-col`, and the plasticity chart with steps 5 and 6 in the right — the figures stay where they are and each keeps its own reasoning beside it, which is the whole point of the split.

**Step 1 blanks the boundary diameter as well as the reading.** $C_F$ is not a number you look up, it is a number you *choose where to look up*, and the choice is $0.002\ \mathrm{mm}$. Blanking only the $20\ \%$ would let a student copy a value off the figure's own annotation without ever engaging with the definition. Two short blue fills: $0.002$ and $20$.

**Step 2 is a whole step for one subtraction, deliberately.** $PI = LL - PL$ is trivial arithmetic, but the statement gives $PL$ and $LL$ while the activity formula wants $PI$, and skipping the conversion — dividing $LL$ by $C_F$, or $PL$ — is the single commonest error in this example. Making it a numbered step with the symbolic form pre-printed in black forces the substitution to be written.

**Step 3 blanks the substitution and the result, and the classification separately.** $\tfrac{31}{20}$ and $1.55$ are both short and both worth writing; the word "active" is a third, small blue fill, because reading a number against the three-band table is a decision and a student who computes $1.55$ and stops has answered only half of part (b).

**Step 4 pre-prints the two contour values in black and blanks only the verdict.** $A = 1.28$ and $A = 1.98$ are what a student would read off the chart's two curves at $C_F = 20\ \%$, and printing them turns an eyeballed lookup into an explicit comparison, which is both more teachable and more checkable. The blue fill is the bracket $1.5\ \% < S_P < 5\ \%$ and the green `.ans` is **Medium**.

**Step 5 blanks both line equations, not just their values.** This is the step that corrects the deck, so it has to be done properly: $PI_A = 0.73(LL-20)$ and $PI_U = 0.9(LL-8)$ are pre-printed symbolically in black, the substitutions $0.73(43-20)$ and $0.9(43-8)$ are blue, and the two margins $+14.2$ and $-0.5$ are blue. Only when both numbers are on the page is "it is a U-line soil" something the student can see rather than be told.

**Step 6 is the result line and carries the only green `.ans` of part 2: the mineral name.** The corroboration from the activity table is black scaffolding beside it, not a blank — it is a confirmation, and asking a student to fill in a confirmation of an answer they have just given teaches nothing.

Red notes, at most one per step. (1) clay *fraction* is a size class — a $20\ \%$ clay fraction says nothing yet about whether any clay mineral is present; that is part (d)'s job. (2) the statement gives $PL$, the formula wants $PI$ — convert before dividing. (3) the deck's three activity bands are $<0.75$, $0.75$–$1.25$, $>1.25$; the boundaries are not $1$. (4) the chart is zoned, not gridded — you get a band between two contours, so report a class and not a single number. (5) **the two lines mean different minerals** — just above the A-line is illite, just below the U-line is montmorillonite; compute both before deciding. (6) the U-line is an upper bound on real soils, so a point *above* it means a bad test, not an exotic mineral.

**Two notes need `data-slide` short forms.** Step 1: "Clay *fraction* = percent finer than $0.002$ mm. A size class, not a mineral — (d) asks the mineral." Step 5: "Just **above** the A-line $\to$ illite; just **below** the U-line $\to$ montmorillonite. Work out both."
