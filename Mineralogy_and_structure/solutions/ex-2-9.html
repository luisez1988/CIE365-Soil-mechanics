Surface area of the original cube \(A_o=\) \(6L^{2}\) A cube has six faces
Volume and mass of the original cube \(V_o=\) \(L^{3}\) \(\implies\) \(m_o=V_o\rho=\) \(L^{3}\rho\)
Original specific surface \(SSA_o=\dfrac{A_o}{V_o\rho}=\) \(\dfrac{6L^{2}}{L^{3}\rho}\) \(=\) \(\dfrac{6}{L\rho}\)
For the halved particle \(L_f=\dfrac{L}{2}\), so \(A_f=6 \left(\dfrac{L}{2} \right)^{2}=\) \(\dfrac{3L^{2}}{2}\)  and  \(V_f=\left(\dfrac{L}{2}\right)^{3}=\) \(\dfrac{L^{3}}{8}\) \(\left(\tfrac12\right)^{2}=\tfrac14\) but \(\left(\tfrac12\right)^{3}=\tfrac18\). Both are smaller, but the volume falls faster.
New specific surface \(SSA_f=\dfrac{A_f}{V_f\rho}=\) \(\dfrac{3L^{2}/2}{(L^{3}/8)\,\rho}\) \(=\) \(\dfrac{12}{L\rho}\) \(SSA\) is area per unit mass, so \(\unit{m^2/kg}\). A definition using \(A/V\) instead gives \(\unit{m^{-1}}\) and the same factor.
By what factor the specific surface changes \(\dfrac{SSA_f}{SSA_o}= \) \(\dfrac{12/(L\rho)}{6/(L\rho)}\)= 2  \(\implies\) the specific surface doubles
In general \(A\propto L^{2}\) and \(V\propto L^{3}\), so \(SSA\propto\dfrac{1}{L}\) for any shape held geometrically similar.
