## Statement

> For problem 6.1; determine the time required to obtain 95% degree of consolidation if the clay's coefficient of consolidation is $C_v=15$ m$^2$/yr (Boston's blue clay) or $C_v=0.4$ m$^2$/yr (Mexico City clay). In both cases plot settlement vs. time.

"Problem 6.1" is Example 7.1 in the Consolidation deck, which uses the same profile, `FiguresGeneral/Profile_ex61.svg`. Its approved solution is `Consolidation/solutions/ex-7-1.md`.

## Given / Find

- From Example 7.1: $\delta_{pc} = 0.509\ \mathrm{m}$, clay thickness $H_0 = 10\ \mathrm{m}$, with $9\ \mathrm{m}$ of sand above and coarse sand and gravel below
- $C_v = 15\ \mathrm{m^2/yr}$ (Boston blue clay) and $C_v = 0.4\ \mathrm{m^2/yr}$ (Mexico City clay)
- $U = 95\%$
- **Find:** $t_{95}$ for each clay, and $\delta$ against $t$ for each clay

## Assumptions

The clay drains at both faces, into the sand above and the gravel below, so $H_{dr} = H_0/2 = 5\ \mathrm{m}$.

The load is applied at once, and the initial excess pore pressure is uniform through the clay. These are the conditions for the analytical solution and its approximations.

$\delta_{pc} = 0.509\ \mathrm{m}$ is taken from the approved Example 7.1. The handwritten solution uses $0.513\ \mathrm{m}$; see Flags.

## Solution

The settlement at any time follows from the degree of consolidation, $\delta(t) = \delta_{pc}\,U(t)/100$, and time enters through $T_v = \dfrac{C_v t}{H_{dr}^2}$. These are the two Principles on the handwritten slide. For a target $U$, find $T_v$ and solve for $t$. For a given $t$, find $T_v$ and then $U$.

1. Drainage length — double drainage, so $H_{dr} = \dfrac{H_0}{2} = \dfrac{10}{2} = 5\ \mathrm{m}$
2. Normalized time for 95% — from Example 7.5 or the table, $T_v = 1.129$ for $U = 95\%$
3. Time for 95%, Boston blue clay — $t = \dfrac{T_v H_{dr}^2}{C_v} = \dfrac{1.129 \times (5)^2}{15} = \mathbf{1.88\ \mathrm{yr}}$
4. Time for 95%, Mexico City clay — $t = \dfrac{1.129 \times (5)^2}{0.4} = \mathbf{70.6\ \mathrm{yr}}$
5. Settlement vs. time — use a list of known times, for example $t = 0$ to $75\ \mathrm{yr}$. A sample calculation for $t = 25\ \mathrm{yr}$ follows in steps 5a and 5b.
   - 5a. Boston blue clay — $T_v = \dfrac{C_v t}{H_{dr}^2} = \dfrac{15 \times 25}{25} = 15$. $T_v > 0.2$, so $U = \left[1 - \dfrac{8}{\pi^2}\exp\left(-\dfrac{\pi^2 T_v}{4}\right)\right] \times 100 = \left[1 - 0.811\exp\left(-\dfrac{9.87 \times 15}{4}\right)\right] \times 100 = 100\%$, and $\delta(25\ \mathrm{yr}) = \dfrac{100 \times 0.509}{100} = \mathbf{0.509\ \mathrm{m}}$
   - 5b. Mexico City clay — $T_v = \dfrac{0.4 \times 25}{25} = 0.4$. From the table or the same equation, $U = 69.8\%$, and $\delta(25\ \mathrm{yr}) = \dfrac{69.8 \times 0.509}{100} = \mathbf{0.355\ \mathrm{m}}$
6. Repeat for every time in the list. The table and the plot below are the result.
